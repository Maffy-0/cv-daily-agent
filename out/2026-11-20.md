## 1. Look Closer, Look Back, or Wait for Agentic Streaming Video Understanding
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24267v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24267v1
- Authors: Pengyu Li, Zhitao Gao, Lingling Zhang, Wenxuan Huang, YiCheng Xiao, Chen Zheng, Jun Liu
- Keyword score: 7 / hits: streaming, real-time, video understanding

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Streaming video models must answer questions about a live video under strict memory and latency limits. Feeding only the latest few frames keeps present perception sharp, whereas adding past frames dilutes attention on the current scene and triggers hallucinations. Recent cascade methods retrieve past frames when uncertain, but they still fail when current frames are blurry or when decisive future clues have not appeared yet. We introduce ActiveForager, a streaming video agent that maintains a clean short working window and gathers extra evidence only on demand. For each query, the agent either answers directly, zooms into high-resolution recent frames, recalls past events from memory, or waits for upcoming frames. When recalling history, a shared-weight sub-agent verifies candidate clips in isolation and returns a compact evidence summary. A stream-decoupled attention mask then keeps historical evidence from interfering with present visual tokens. We train this policy using counterfactual information gain, which rewards a tool call only when the gathered evidence makes the true answer more likely and prevents tool collapse across all actions. Across Qwen2.5-VL-7B and Qwen3-VL-8B on OVO-Bench, ActiveForager improves real-time perception, historical recall, and proactive response at once, lifting overall accuracy on Qwen3-VL-8B from 47.6% to 65.9%. It also doubles zero-shot accuracy on the multi-hop Video-Holmes benchmark while adding minimal visual tokens per query.

</details>

## 2. $Z$-sight: Unifying Vision-Language-Action and Latent World Modeling
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24940v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24940v1
- Authors: Junjie Ye, Yiqi Zhao, Muchen Xu, Celina Shiyu Wang, Hao Dong, Ruohai Ge, Rong Xue, Benjamin J Kolligs...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We introduce $Z$-sight, a unified robot foundation model that couples vision-language-action (VLA) and generative latent world modeling. Video-based world action models (WAMs) show the value of joint future-and-action generation for control, yet inherit the heavy cost of video generators. Recent latent-space policies avoid pixel generation, yet typically align intermediate features or rely on frozen target encoders, decoupling future prediction from the VLM's native feature space. $Z$-sight instead predicts the future directly in the visual token space in which its VLM perceives the present. Coupling a foresight expert and a continuous action expert with the VLM in a mixture-of-transformers, $Z$-sight jointly denoises future spatial tokens and actions at inference without pixel reconstruction or external encoders. Trained on human video and robot trajectories, $Z$-sight achieves 81.9% success on SIMPLE (+9.6 points over the strongest baseline) and 73.3% across six Unitree G1 tasks (+20.0 points) at 110 ms per chunk, while remaining competitive on tabletop benchmarks.

</details>

## 3. Kaer: Security-Aware Replanning for Multi-Agent Systems
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24839v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24839v1
- Authors: Tushin Mallick, Evan Rose, Matthew Laws, Alina Oprea, Cristina Nita-Rotaru
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

One popular architecture for multi-agent systems (MAS) relies on an orchestrator that generates an initial plan, coordinates its execution, and dynamically revises the plan based on agent feedback when tasks cannot be fully specified in advance. This dynamic replanning introduces a critical vulnerability: malicious agents can embed instructions within their feedback to hijack the replanning process. Existing defenses focus exclusively on per-tool access control or static, pre-execution policy enforcement, leaving the replanning phase unsecured. We introduce Kaer, a multi-agent framework featuring security-aware replanning. Kaer shields the planner from untrusted agent feedback by conveying diagnostics to the replanning procedure strictly through abstract templates. Plan revisions are restricted to a set of deterministic repair operators, and all modified plans must satisfy preset policies. We create Cintra, a benchmark of simulated user tasks requiring replanning paired with adversarial objectives targeting the replanning phase. Using Cintra, we evaluate both task completion rates and system vulnerability to replanning exploits by malicious agents. Evaluating Kaer on Cintra against Magentic-One (an undefended MAS) and ControlValve (the state-of-the-art defense) demonstrates that Kaer achieves higher robustness than both systems while maintaining comparable utility to ControlValve. Although abstract failure templates slightly constrain utility on select tasks, Kaer enables MAS to perform secure, dynamic plan revisions without exposing the orchestrator to untrusted agent feedback.

</details>

## 4. Towards Pose-Robust Few-Shot Vision-Language-Action Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24360v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24360v1
- Authors: Wenhao Li, Zhongyi Han, Yuhao Wei, Liang Han, Yilong Yin, Liqiang Nie
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Fine-tuning a vision-language-action (VLA) model on a few dozen demonstrations is the standard way to put a manipulation policy on a robot, yet the resulting policy silently assumes that the camera and the robot base stay exactly where they were during data collection: a bumped tripod or a base re-docked a few centimetres away makes it reach for the wrong place. We show that both failures are one phenomenon, a change of the camera-to-base extrinsic that the policy has memorised, and that the robot carries everything needed to undo it. We introduce WACO, a world-anchored canonicalization framework for VLA models: the arm, placed by forward kinematics at the measured joint angles, serves as a metric calibration target, and the static scene serves as the world anchor that tells a moved camera from a moved robot. From a single RGB-D frame, WACO estimates both shifts without ground-truth extrinsics or extra data, canonicalises the image to the calibration viewpoint and the state, action and start pose to the calibration base frame, and fine-tunes the unmodified policy under the artefacts of its own estimator. We also introduce LIBERO-PoseShift, the first benchmark for RGB VLAs with graded camera, base and compound shifts. Across LIBERO-PoseShift, the LIBERO-Plus camera track and four real-world tasks on a UR5e robot, WACO consistently outperforms the standard few-shot recipe and prior viewpoint-robust methods, raising shifted success by 13.2 points in simulation and by 36.7 points on the real robot, recovering 90% of the simulation gap to a ground-truth oracle and 87% of the real-robot gap, and establishing a new state of the art among methods that use no perturbed training data, with no test-time calibration. Project page: https://peacelwh.github.io/WACO/.

</details>

## 5. OCTR2A: Real-Time RGB-to-BEV Trajectory Projection and Forecasting at Urban Intersections
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24352v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24352v1
- Authors: Emanuele Iacobelli, Simone Teglia, Francesco Pro, Claudia Melis Tonti, Irene Amerini
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Trajectory forecasting at urban intersections is often studied assuming that reliable bird's-eye-view (BEV) motion histories are available, which in practice requires costly resources such as LiDAR, aerial views, HD maps, or calibrated infrastructure. We address this upstream perception-to-forecasting problem by reconstructing and predicting agents' BEV trajectories only from paired opposite-corner RGB cameras mounted on existing urban infrastructure at variable heights at inference time. We introduce the Opposite-Corner Trajectory Reconstruction and Anticipation Architecture (OCTR2A), a modular pipeline integrating per-view detection with class refinement, geometric cross-camera matching, transformer-based BEV projection, and egocentric GRU forecasting, trained in cascade and mainly supervised with a soft multi-scale spatial-overlap loss. Since no public benchmark provides this sensing configuration, we introduce R3ST++, a Blender-based synthetic benchmark built from SIND trajectories that provides synchronized opposite-corner views, cross-view correspondences, and BEV ground truth, while also serving as a deployment-support simulator for site-specific fine-tuning. Experiments on these datasets show that, with 12 observed and 12 predicted frames at 10\,Hz, OCTR2A achieves projection~-~forecasting ADE/FDE of 0.144/0.140~-~0.154/0.241~meters in real time on a single consumer GPU. Evaluated on ground-truth BEV histories, the forecasting module improves over the strongest state-of-the-art methods on SIND by 66.7\%/41.7\% ADE/FDE. Transfer experiments on TUMTraf-V2X show that R3ST++ pretraining supports adaptation to real-world roadside domains with different sensing configurations.

</details>

## 6. Old Experience, New Lessons: Relabeling Autonomous Robot Rollouts in Interactive Imitation Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24273v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.24273v1
- Authors: Zhaoting Li, Cosimo Della Santina, Jens Kober
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: imitation learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Interactive imitation learning enables robots to learn from human interventions during deployment. These interactions naturally produce sparse human corrections and abundant autonomous robot experience without human action labels. Yet existing methods either leave this experience unused for action supervision or imitate historical robot actions, which can reinforce suboptimal behavior. To address this limitation, we propose Set-valued Relabeling of Autonomous experience (SeRA), a framework that learns from autonomous robot experience through set-valued supervision of policy relabels. Later policy checkpoints relabel this experience, and agreement across checkpoints filters the relabels. Since agreement does not guarantee correctness, SeRA constructs desired action sets around accepted relabels. Following Set-Supervised Diffusion Policy (SDP), it trains an action-chunking diffusion policy to increase the probability of actions within these sets. A step-masked denoising loss combines accepted relabels with available human corrections in partially supervised chunks. Experiments across five simulated manipulation tasks in online and offline settings show that SeRA improves policy performance over SDP and outperforms baselines that imitate historical robot actions or exact relabels. On a real-robot tomato-picking task, SeRA achieves 80% success, compared with 40% for SDP, using the same human feedback and training budget. These results demonstrate that relabeled autonomous robot experience can complement human corrections to improve policy performance.

</details>

## 8. RECLAIM: Robot-Vision-Informed Adversarial Patch Defense via Embodied Attention Feedback

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-language-action policies control embodied agents through a perception-action loop grounded in physical interaction, where adversarial patches can corrupt action predictions and thereby alter subsequent observations. To mitigate these threats, many existing defenses rely on policy adaptation or auxiliary models, but require additional training or inference. We introduce RECLAIM, a training-free, inference-time defense that uses a frozen VLA's own attention response to local visual interventions to guide purification during closed-loop embodied manipulation. Our key insight is that attention changes induced by local edits provide feedback for choosing where and how to purify as the robot interacts with its environment. Under adversarial patch attacks, RECLAIM succeeds in at least one rollout on 95% of the evaluated tasks and raises rollout success on LIBERO-Object from 0% without defense to 70.0%. Its mean decision time is only 548.9 ms per control step. These results highlight policy-guided visual purification as an effective approach to defending embodied agents against adversarial patches and recovering closed-loop task execution.

## 9. Cap-and-Track: Optimizing LLM Inference Request Dispatch Across Shifting Capacity
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.24891v1

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

A fixed dispatch rate for LLM inference becomes stale as serving fleets resize and request sizes change. Generative output length is unknown at admission, and shared provider pools can lose capacity.
