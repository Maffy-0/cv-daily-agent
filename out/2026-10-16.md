## 1. Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning with Human Interventions for Precise Robotic Manipulation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20504v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20504v1
- Authors: Xiaoman Li, Zhiqiang Yang, Jiayu Hu, Jipeng Kong, Yang Zhang, Jingwei Zhang, Fangzheng Yan, Chunhua Shen...
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: vision-language-action, behavior cloning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-horizon precise manipulation produces mixed-quality data for post-training vision-language-action (VLA) models: even successful demonstrations and intervention-assisted rollouts may contain detours, hesitations, failed attempts, and imperfect recoveries. The key challenge is to identify which action chunks actually advance the task, rather than uniformly cloning or discarding imperfect trajectories. We propose Graph-Reweighted Behavior Cloning (GR-BC), an offline iterative post-training framework that builds a task-conditioned goal-reachability graph over demonstrations and iteratively collected rollouts, including both successful and failed episodes with human interventions. Reverse graph search computes minimal remaining steps to task success within the dataset support, and each action chunk is weighted by how much its execution reduces this potential. This consequence-based weighting reuses recoverable segments from failed trajectories, leverages interventions as recovery paths, and down-weights locally regressive chunks even inside successful trajectories. New rollouts are incorporated by appending them to the graph and rerunning search, without training a reward model, action-conditioned critic, or progress predictor. Across four precise manipulation tasks and three VLA backbones, GR-BC improves single-round supervised fine-tuning and reaches 91% success after a few offline iterations, without trajectory comparisons or progress labels. More details and videos are available at: https://gr-bc.github.io

</details>

## 2. AdaRSI: On-Demand Recursive Self-Improvement for Embodied Agents
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20474v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20474v1
- Authors: Peidong Wang, Ying Chang, Zhiming Ma, Xiaocui Yang, Shi Feng, Daqi Liu, Yuzhe Yang, Xu Cao...
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: vision-language-action, embodied agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Pretrained vision-language-action models (VLAs) often fail midway through unseen long-horizon tasks. Recursive self-improvement (RSI) offers a remedy, but embodied RSI methods either refine the harness around a frozen executor and so cannot add a missing motion, or train the executor on persistent failures without asking whether a failure lies in how the executor is called or in what it can do. We ask when a self-improving embodied system should change the weights of its executor, and propose AdaRSI, an on-demand RSI framework for embodied agents. A coding agent first improves its skills and harness, then attributes persistent failures by retrying failing sub-steps from saved states, and fine-tunes only the sub-steps that remain unreliable, with demonstrations it collects itself and experience carried over from earlier tasks. On all 50 target tasks of RoboCasa365, with GR00T N1.5 as the executor and no human demonstrations, AdaRSI improves the VLA on all three suites and raises its success on unseen composite tasks from 4.6% to 13.8%. Compared with fine-tuning every sub-step, it reaches slightly higher success while using only 28% of the GPU time and 22% of the self-collected demonstrations, and fine-tuning without a harness trains more yet gains far less.

</details>

## 3. StreamErase: Towards Ultra-Fast Streaming Video Erasing
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20137v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20137v1
- Authors: Zhuobai Dong, Xiaobing Tu, Xiantao Zhang, Jinkui Ren, Yinggui Wang, Yue Ma, Linfeng Zhang
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: streaming, real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Current video object removal methods suffer from prohibitive latency (minutes per video) and non-causal processing that prevents real-time interaction. We present StreamErase, enabling sub-second latency with up to 26 FPS streaming generation on a single GPU. Our approach first augments a text-to-video model with mask input to generate high-quality videos that erase target objects and synthesize coherent backgrounds, but does not perform inference on the fly. As such, we progressively transfer the erasing capability from a powerful bidirectional foundation model to an efficient unidirectional streaming eraser, enabling long-horizon erasing without sacrificing visual fidelity. Several key challenges arise when generating videos over long, potentially infinite horizons: (1) bridging the domain gap from training on finite length and extrapolating to long horizons, (2) sustaining high quality by preventing error accumulation, and (3) maintaining fast inference, without increasing computational cost as the context grows. To address these challenges, we carefully designed sliding-window causal attention with attention sinks. By incorporating self-rollout with attention sinks and KV cache rolling during training, we simulate inference-time extrapolations with a fixed context window, enabling constant-speed generation of arbitrarily long videos. Finally, we establish a dedicated benchmark, LongErase, for streaming long-video object removal. Our model achieves strong performance compared to existing video object erasing models and generalizes well to real-world video scenarios, uniquely enabling infinite-length streaming. With StreamErase, users can input a mask and see the erasing results unfold in real-time, delivering a truly interactive experience.

## 4. Physical Time as a Missing Dimension of Robot Policy Memory Dynamics
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20524v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20524v1
- Authors: Yushen Liang, Yue Peng, Baosheng Jin, Tianluo Zhang, Xinyu Zhang, Shuyi Zhou, Zhuoran Chen, Xinqi Liu...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: robot policy

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Existing robot policy memories typically evolve with observation sequences, implicitly treating each update as the same amount of physical time. Yet identical observation histories can span different durations and require different actions, exposing physical time as a missing dimension of policy memory. We introduce Temporal Flow Policy (TFP), which learns memory dynamics to retain past events and evolve the memory state over measured time intervals. Observations and prior memory determine the content and time scales of each transition; elapsed time determines how far the state advances. TFP conditions diffusion and flow-matching policies through this memory state and is trained with their native imitation objectives. We evaluate TFP with Diffusion Policy and $π_{0.5}$ through controlled comparisons of memory designs. These experiments show that successful timing depends on both measured intervals and retained history. TFP achieves higher average success than the recurrent and state-space memories we equip with the same intervals and than a variant that feeds the interval to a learned gate. Memory readouts recover the time since an event and motion information after occlusion. Changing the supplied intervals systematically delays or advances actions, linking time inputs to behavior. Real-robot tasks requiring timing or memory, together with LIBERO evaluations, demonstrate benefits in physical and general manipulation. These results suggest viewing robot memory not as stored observation history, but as a state that retains past events and evolves with physical time.

</details>

## 5. Safe Reinforcement Learning with Receding-Horizon Safety Critic and Differentiable Action Shields
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20255v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.20255v1
- Authors: Siwei Ju, Jan Peters, Oleg Arenz
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Despite the Markov assumption, safety failures in robot reinforcement learning rarely occur instantaneously; they typically emerge over several steps as the policy enters unfamiliar states under disturbances and domain shift. We address this limitation by learning a receding-horizon safety critic that predicts whether a state will remain non-terminating over the next receding horizon, enabling anticipatory detection of impending locomotion failures. The critic is trained from rollout-derived horizon labels with a composite objective that combines horizon classification, control-barrier-function-inspired temporal consistency, and smoothness regularization, and we aggregate an ensemble of critics with a conservative Conditional Value at Risk estimate to account for uncertainty. Building on the learned critic, we derive a closed-form, differentiable CBF-style safety filter that minimally modifies the policy action to improve predicted safety, and integrate it into PPO with an anticipation loss to reduce policy-filter mismatch during training. In simulated quadruped locomotion, our method improves robustness under action noise, external perturbations, and terrain shifts, reducing catastrophic failures while maintaining competitive returns.

</details>

## 12. Co-Evolving Acting and Forecasting for Language Agents
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.20597v1

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Training language agents is bottlenecked by the high cost of long-horizon rollouts, yet standard agentic reinforcement learning (RL) learns only from a terminal scalar reward, leaving the supervision in intermediate steps largely unused. Prior work exploits this supervision through value targets that estimate per-step success probabilities or observation targets that fit environment response. Both are hard to construct well: (1) value targets come from the same single scalar signal for each step in the trajectory, and (2) observation targets, such as web pages and terminal logs, are long and noisy and reach only one step ahead.
