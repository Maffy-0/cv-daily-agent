## 1. Learning to Steer LLM Agents and Multi-Agent Systems
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22316v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22316v1
- Authors: Leyang Shen, Yaxin Gong, Yang Zhang, Chenxiao Fan, Jiakai Wang, Dong Wang, Yang Liu, Fuli Feng...
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: reinforcement learning, multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Large language model (LLM)-based agents and multi-agent systems increasingly tackle complex tasks through long-horizon tool use and inter-agent collaboration. However, these systems are prone to deviating from the overall goal as execution unfolds, which wastes execution resources and leads to undesired outcomes, a failure termed goal drift. In this work, we propose a steering agent that supervises execution on the fly and steers the system back on track when drift occurs. The steering agent observes the execution context, deciding when to intervene with a low-cost fast path and how to intervene with a high-cost slow path. To unlock its steering intelligence, the ability to assess goal drift and generate effective interventions, we jointly train the two paths with reinforcement learning, combining a pairwise ranking objective with group-relative policy optimization (GRPO). Experiments on information-seeking search tasks show that the steering agent provides effective supervision for both single-agent and multi-agent systems, improving their performance and efficiency. The learned steering intelligence generalizes to out-of-domain tasks, transfers to unseen executors, and supports controllable intervention frequency without retraining.

</details>

## 2. Auditable and Deployable Multi-Agent Motion Planning via Posetal Games from Legal Guidelines
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22277v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22277v1
- Authors: Florian Lercher, Karan Mahesh, Yujun Huang, Meshal Alharbi, Runyu Zhang, Hanna Krasowski, Michael R. Benjamin, Matthias Althoff...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

When robots are deployed in the real-world, they must interact with other agents and accomplish their own goals while adhering to legal constraints. These potentially conflicting objectives are often only partially prioritized, making scalarized objectives or fully specified hierarchies unsuitable. We present a framework for multi-agent motion planning under legal requirements based on games with partially ordered preferences (posetal games). Scenario-independent legal guidelines order abstract legal concerns, from which we automatically derive agent preferences and a legal game for any concrete scenario. Agents may refine their legal preferences with private objectives, and regulators may refine the guideline as laws evolve. We prove that such customizations can only remove equilibria and thus preserve legality. To coordinate agents without explicitly communicating private preferences, we introduce belief play, which provably recovers the true equilibria from conservative local games, and a distributed iterative best-response protocol requiring less information. We apply the framework to maritime navigation, systematically lifting pairwise traffic rules to multi-vessel encounters. On seven encounters with up to four vessels, every rule violation of the committed equilibria is certified as forced by the legal hierarchy or unsatisfiable, and defectors are identified from public information in all tested cases. These results indicate that guideline-based posetal games offer an auditable route from partially specified laws to multi-agent motion planning.

</details>

## 3. Robust Multi-Agent Pursuit with Preemptive Communication via Reachability-Based Control Barrier Functions
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22198v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22198v1
- Authors: Madison Book, Justin Bradley
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Safe multi-agent coordination typically assumes what real-world, deployed, resource-constrained robots cannot provide: continuous, perfect state sharing. Robust control strategies must be complemented with robust communication strategies to deliver resilient deployments. In this work, we propose a unified control/communication strategy that provides safe control to guarantee inter-agent safety under intermittent communication and demonstrate its effectiveness in a pursuit-evasion scenario. Each agent computes the reachable set of neighboring agents based on the last state information received, then solves for a safe control input based on control barrier functions to ensure agents avoid collision. We prove that inter-agent collision avoidance is guaranteed provided communication staleness remains bounded. As the reachable set inflates between state transmissions, each agent reasons about when to preemptively transmit its state information to neighboring agents based on novel metrics characterizing uncertainty risk and kinematic risk. We implement and simulate a variety of pursuit-evasion scenarios and show that preemptive communication reduces messages by 77.5% compared to periodic and event-triggered baselines with zero collisions.

</details>

## 4. Specification Gaming and a Constrained Reinforcement Learning Mitigation in a Cleaning Gridworld
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22152v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22152v1
- Authors: Hilda Adwubi Osei
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Reinforcement learning agents optimize exactly the reward signal they are given, which can diverge sharply from an intended true objective when that signal is only a proxy for it; this failure mode is known as specification gaming. Existing evidence for this failure mode is largely observational, with comparatively few demonstrations pairing a controlled, deliberately constructed proxy/true gap with a mitigation that carries a verifiable guarantee rather than only an empirical improvement. We construct a minimal gridworld in which a cleaning-robot agent can either genuinely remove dirt or conceal it from a cheap sensor, and show that a standard deep Q-network trained only on the sensor's proxy reward converges to a policy that games it completely, reaching near-maximal proxy reward while never achieving the true objective. We then introduce a constrained reinforcement learning fix, penalizing the specific behaviors through which the agent exploits the gap, and show that it eliminates the gaming behavior from the final policy while substantially recovering true-objective performance. We further derive and empirically verify an exact accounting identity relating the proxy reward, true reward, and our cost signal for this environment, showing that the cost signal measures the proxy/true gap exactly rather than merely tracking it, and yields a deterministic guarantee under a hard-constrained variant of the method.

</details>

## 5. FastVLA: Task-Conditioned and Temporally Adaptive Visual Token Compression for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22146v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.22146v1
- Authors: Jiuyi Xu, Chengyuan Qian, Zhengzhong Tu, Yang Sui, Yangming Shi
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models present a promising paradigm for general-purpose robot manipulation, but closed-loop deployment is often limited by inference efficiency: at each timestep, high-resolution, often multi-view images are encoded into hundreds of visual tokens that dominate transformer computation and latency. Token compression via attention is a natural solution but is challenging for VLA control because (1) decision-critical tokens are determined by the task, not generic visual saliency, and (2) the token budget varies substantially across steps, so static metrics and budgets waste computation or discard control-critical tokens. We introduce FastVLA, a training-free, task-aware, and temporally adaptive visual token compression framework. At each step, FastVLA scores token importance via task-to-vision attention and adaptively sets the compression rate from the importance-distribution uniformity, measured by an inverse-Simpson metric with online sliding-window calibration. We evaluate FastVLA on OpenVLA, OpenVLA-OFT, and CogACT across LIBERO and SIMPLER, and on a physical Franka Research 3 robot. In simulation, it reduces LLM-backbone FLOPs by 35-59\% and action latency by 9-32\% (a 1.09$\times$-1.46$\times$ speedup) while maintaining task success, achieving a comparable or better efficiency-performance trade-off than existing plug-and-play token compression/pruning/reuse baselines. On the real robot, it cuts end-to-end latency by 20\% (1.25$\times$ speedup) at comparable success. Code is available at https://github.com/jiuyixu25/FastVLA.

</details>

## 6. What Should Robot Policies Learn to Predict? Rethinking Supervision Targets in Vision-Language-Action Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21915v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21915v1
- Authors: Haitao Shen, Hanyu Liu, Yihan Lin, Huiping Liu, Junbo Cui, Daoheng Li, Yang Li, Jing Zhang...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies are typically trained with action prediction as the primary supervision signal, since robots ultimately execute actions. Auxiliary objectives such as latent-action (LA) and future-vision (FV) prediction are often added to improve policy learning, yet they compete with action prediction for the same training budget. We ask whether, under a fixed budget, allocating a substantial share of training samples to such objectives, rather than to further action prediction, can improve policy learning. FV generally outperforms LA, although the strongest LA target remains competitive on CALVIN. Diagnostic analyses suggest that DINO-based LA targets emphasize relative motion, whereas FV targets emphasize absolute position and task identity. We therefore argue that what robot policies learn to predict, and how training is allocated among these targets, should be treated as explicit design choices rather than defaulting to action-dominant training.

## 7. Diffeomorphic Hidden-State Steering: Improving Imitation Learning Policy Robustness through Expert Hidden-State Geometry
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.21666v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.21666v1
- Authors: KwangBin Lee
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: imitation learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Small observation or action errors can compound during closed-loop execution and disrupt a trained imitation learning policy. We study whether the original expert demonstrations can improve robustness through test-time hidden-state steering without additional demonstrations or policy retraining. Our analysis of a trained policy finds that expert hidden-state variation is concentrated in relatively few dimensions, while the directions capturing this variation differ across regions. Motivated by this geometry, we propose Diffeomorphic Hidden-State Steering (DHS), which learns an invertible nonlinear coordinate map from expert hidden states obtained by replaying the demonstrations through the frozen policy. DHS attenuates coordinates with low expert variance more strongly than those with high variance, then maps the modified coordinates back to hidden-state space. This allows steering directions to vary with the current hidden state while retaining every coordinate. Across four simulated manipulation tasks, DHS improves mean success under observation noise over the unmodified Transformer policies. Controlled comparisons yield higher mean success with learned nonlinear coordinates than with linear coordinates under both hard projection and full-rank rescaling. Combining DHS with action smoothing improves mean success rate under observation noise in certain tasks compared with action smoothing alone.

</details>

## 12. Speculative Forcing for Efficient Autoregressive Video Generation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22347v1

- Authors: Hyunwook Choi, Seungryong Kim, Dongyoon Han, Sangdoo Yun
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: streaming

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Few-step autoregressive video generators enable streaming generation, yet they run every token through the final denoising step, regardless of how much refinement it needs. We find this uniform computation is often unnecessary: many tokens already closely match their eventual full-step outputs in the initial steps, while others require refinement until the end. We introduce Speculative Forcing, a framework that treats intermediate clean estimates as drafts and predicts when individual tokens can be safely accepted early.
