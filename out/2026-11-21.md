## 1. FlexVSR: Continuous-Scale Super-Resolution for Streaming Video Generation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26178v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26178v1
- Authors: Ji Wang, Haonan An, Jiasheng Zhang, Renlong Chen
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: streaming, real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Real-time streaming video generation has gained rapid momentum in live content creation and interactive media. Deploying such systems in practice requires flexible scaling factors to accommodate diverse source resolutions, target displays, and computational budgets. However, extending fixed-scale generative video super-resolution (VSR) to varying target scales poses two challenges: downsampling from a larger fixed scale incurs redundant cost, while re-optimizing the diffusion transformer (DiT) is costly and alters the shared generative prior. To address both, we propose FlexVSR, a decoupled framework for continuous-scale streaming generative VSR with supervision at only a few scales. Our key insight is that naively resampling fixed-scale latents onto a new target grid induces scale-dependent input mismatch, which can be effectively addressed through parameter-efficient, scale-aware adaptation of the latent interface without updating the DiT. To this end, FlexVSR introduces ReLSA (Refined Latent Scale Adaptation), a lightweight latent adapter that constructs and refines target-grid latents before they enter the DiT, enabling a shared frozen VSR prior to generalize across continuous scales with only 0.274M trainable parameters and an adaptation cost of 7.3 H100 GPU-hours, while introducing no additional temporal dependencies and preserving the native causal streaming protocol. Extensive experiments demonstrate competitive or superior reconstruction and temporal quality, lower latency than most native-scale streaming baselines and offline methods, and effective generalization to unseen continuous scales, filling the continuous-scale gap in real-time streaming generative VSR.

</details>

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Exocentric-to-egocentric (Exo2Ego) video generation transforms third-person recordings into first-person viewpoints, unlocking immersive AR/VR experiences and scalable demonstration synthesis for embodied robotics. Extending it to a streaming setting is expected to further enable live applications. However, existing methods suffer from degraded manipulation regions, overfitting and confinement to fixed-length clips with prohibitive latency. To address these limitations, we present StreamEgo, a real-time framework for unbounded, generalizable Exo2Ego video generation. We also introduce a region-aware loss curriculum to improve generalization and stabilize multi-objective diffusion training. Furthermore, we distill the model into a chunk-wise autoregressive model, augmented with a region-aware corruption mechanism and a first-chunk curriculum, enabling high-quality, generalizable real-time streaming Exo2Ego generation at 24.3 FPS on a single H100 GPU.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

## 4. PhyDEX: A Benchmark for Physical Reasoning in Vision-Language-Action Models via Dynamic Extrinsic Dexterity

- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.26565v1

- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models have advanced rapidly in language-conditioned robotic manipulation, yet existing benchmarks mainly evaluate gripper--object contact and largely ignore how objects interact with the environment through gravity, friction, inertia, and mass distribution. As a result, it remains unclear whether current policies truly understand physics or merely interpolate from their training data. We address this gap through the lens of dynamic extrinsic dexterity and introduce PhyDEX, a throwing benchmark in which a robot must infer an object's mass, center of mass, and friction to send it to a destination beyond its kinematic workspace. PhyDEX contains 35K trajectories (143 hours) across nine tasks and 100 scenes, covering uniform, irregular, and dynamic-mass objects with static, multiple, and moving destinations. All data are generated by a scalable, fully automatic pipeline that combines grasp generation, motion planning, and closed-loop throw optimization under randomized physical properties. We evaluate three representative VLAs, GR00T N1.7, DynamicVLA, and ForceVLA, and find that all achieve success rates below 3\%. Our analysis reveals two key bottlenecks: limited robustness of pretrained models, which causes most failures before the throw, and physical blindness, as providing ground-truth physical properties roughly doubles success. Experiments on a humanoid robot and in the real world show the same failure patterns. We hope PhyDEX will drive the development of physically grounded robot policies.

## 5. AURA: AUdio-dRiven streaming Avatar
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.26540v1

- Authors: Nathaniel Cohen, Nicolas Dufour, Amélie Royer, Alasdair Newson, Patrick Pérez
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: streaming

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Current audio-driven video diffusion models can synthesize realistic talking-head videos but generally require dozens of diffusion denoising steps over multi-second video chunks, making them unsuitable for streaming applications such as voice assistants and virtual avatars, where low-latency and online causal generation are required properties. AURA generates video chunk by chunk with a rolling KV cache, requiring only the audio of the current chunk to start generating, rather than a multi-second audio look-ahead like bidirectional models. On standard talking-head benchmarks, AURA 1.3B runs 23x faster than its own bidirectional teacher and reaches a quality comparable to TalkVerse at a lower resolution, and AURA 5B matches its TalkVerse teacher at 720p while running 17x faster. To support reproducibility and further research on streaming avatar generation, we release the weights of both models along with a dataset of 30K synthetic videos and their precomputed teacher trajectories.

## 10. FastRoverVLA: Fast Vision-Language-Action Execution for Off-Road Autonomous Driving

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Off-road vision-language-action (VLA) models generate scene-conditioned plans, but frequent trajectory updates can repeatedly invoke expensive multimodal inference. ## 13. Policy Improvement-Guided Replay for Continual Reinforcement Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.25989v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.25989v1
- Authors: Qin-Wen Luo, Haoxuan Gao, Sheng Ren, Xiang Chen, Sheng-Jun Huang
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Continual reinforcement learning (CRL) requires models to acquire new capabilities while retaining performance on previous tasks. A stability-oriented perspective common in conventional continual learning emphasizes knowledge preservation and interference mitigation. Complementing this perspective, we revisit CRL for vision-language models through policy improvement, a fundamental principle of RL, to preserve acquired performance gains. Using the performance difference lemma, we characterize historical performance changes through the current policy's expected advantages on historical task distributions, motivating replay of historical prompts with the current policy. To allocate limited replay memory, we propose Contrastive Likelihood Replay (CoLiR), which contrasts likelihood gains on correct responses from the final task policy with likelihood reductions on incorrect responses from the initial policy.
