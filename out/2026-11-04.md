## 1. KlaskTron: An Open-Source Platform for Physical Adversarial Multi-Agent RL

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Progress in robot learning, particularly physical multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), is currently challenged by a limited availability of accessible, standardized benchmarks. A key barrier is infrastructural: physical platforms are often prohibitively expensive, require specialized facilities, or lack open mechanisms and designs for reproducibility. We introduce KlaskTron, an open-source, low-cost (<\$2500 USD), desktop-scale robotic testbed for adversarial MARL based on the dynamic tabletop game KLASK. We demonstrate that this baseline enables zero-shot policy transfer, with trained agents exhibiting emergent adversarial behaviors. To ensure full reproducibility of our results, all project assets are released publicly: hardware CAD/BOM and assembly documentation at [https://idscethzurich.github.io/klask_hardware], inference software stack at [https://github.com/IDSCETHZurich/klask_software] and the simulation and MARL setup at [https://github.com/IDSCETHZurich/klask_rl].

## 2. DeepWaveRL: Self-Supervised Full Waveform Inversion via Reinforcement Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03264v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03264v1
- Authors: Peng Jin, Yinan Feng, Yinpeng Chen, Youzuo Lin, Sharon Huang
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Full Waveform Inversion (FWI) is a fundamental inverse problem in geophysics to estimate subsurface properties, such as velocity, from seismic measurements. Recently, self-supervised learning methods remove the need for ground-truth velocity maps during training by leveraging a forward operator to simulate seismic data from predictions and backpropagate the gradients of reconstruction loss through it. In this work, we consider a more challenging setting without accessing such gradients. To this end, we incorporate reinforcement learning into self-supervised FWI and propose a framework named DeepWaveRL. Specifically, we reformulate FWI as a policy learning problem, where the policy network predicts velocity maps as actions, and a forward operator is used solely for simulation. A global scalar reward is computed based on data misfit for each prediction and used to weight policy gradients. Thus, learning proceeds without accessing the gradients from the forward operator. Given the indirect and coarse supervision signals, we further identify key strategies to stabilize learning and mitigate the spatial credit assignment bottleneck. Experimental results show that, despite relying entirely on a single scalar reward, DeepWaveRL achieves competitive performance compared to supervised and self-supervised baselines. These findings suggest that our framework offers a flexible and complementary learning paradigm for the FWI research community.

</details>

## 3. GPU-Native Real-Time Textured Mesh Reconstruction with Seam-Aware Multi-View Radiometric Correction
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03033v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03033v1
- Authors: Hiroyuki Sato
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present a GPU-native volumetric-video system for real-time reconstruction of graphics-compatible textured triangle meshes from multiple RGB-D sensors. Existing TSDF systems accelerate fusion and mesh extraction, but extending them to per-frame textured meshing adds CPU mesh processing, CPU-side texturing work, or extra CPU-GPU transfers between stages. After input staging, our method keeps the reconstruction and texturing stages (TSDF fusion, Marching Cubes, connected-component noise removal, view-based texture mapping, inpainting, and radiometric seam correction) resident on the GPU; no intermediate mesh or image data returns to the host between these stages. A fixed-size-array representation of mesh topology gives constant-time neighbor access on the GPU and makes the downstream mesh-level operations data-parallel. Because we texture each face from a single camera, exposure seams that vary smoothly across each image remain; we estimate a per-camera low-frequency gain field and bake it into the atlas on the GPU. On the CMU Panoptic dataset, our method is faster than the baselines at every tested voxel resolution and number of sensors, achieves peak speedups of more than an order of magnitude over CUDA-accelerated nvblox and CPU-based Open3D, and approaches an offline textured-meshing baseline in perceptual re-rendering quality.

## 6. D&A: Decoupling Description and Assessment Enables VLMs as Zero-Shot Robotic Reward Models

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-Language Models (VLMs) hold promise for zero-shot video task-progress estimation in robot learning, yet existing frameworks frequently suffer from confirmation bias, hallucinating progress in failed executions due to implicit success assumptions or relative temporal ranking designs. Naive prompting of frontier models collapses into a binary success detector that fails to capture nuanced, non-linear progress and failures. While post-training approaches can mitigate this through explicit training, they demand expensive real-world robotics data and require computationally intensive post-training for every individual base model. Empirical results demonstrate that our method calibrates zero-shot estimates and mitigates confirmation bias, providing fine-grained, partial progress metrics that accurately penalize failed executions while correctly interpolating successful runs. Being purely prompt-based, D&A generalizes across tasks, embodiments, and benchmarks---matching or surpassing post-trained reward models---and its reward estimates enable efficient downstream reinforcement learning.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Text-to-SQL translates natural language questions into SQL queries, enabling database access without writing SQL. <details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Contact-rich manipulation requires fine-grained control and complex physical interactions with the environment, posing significant challenges for robot policy learning. ## 13. BLPO: Beyond-Local Policy Optimization for Long-Horizon LLM Agents
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.03005v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.03005v1
- Authors: Xuanren Chen, Chongyang Tao, Wenpeng Hu, Yong Wang, Bo Deng, Xinlei Zheng, Shuai Ma
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-horizon tasks require large language model (LLM) agents to coordinate interdependent actions, a capability central to automating complex workflows. Stepwise group-based reinforcement learning provides finer credit than trajectory-level supervision, but reliable comparisons must account for differences in interaction history. Enforcing this consistency within the limited rollouts of each task can leave insufficient support for estimating action preferences. We propose Beyond-Local Policy Optimization (BLPO), which estimates task-progress advantages from experience shared across tasks and training updates. We summarize trajectory prefixes into task-progress states and abstract actions by their function, allowing corresponding decisions from different tasks to contribute to shared estimates. To control bias and noise from heterogeneous tasks, we accumulate context-relative returns in a global memory with balanced aggregation and reliability weighting. These global advantages complement local comparisons over normalized actions and trajectory-level credit without a learned critic or additional rollouts. Experiments on ALFWorld and WebShop with 1.5B and 7B policies show improved task success over the evaluated baselines under matched training budgets. Further analyses show faster learning and shorter trajectories with little computational overhead.

</details>

## 14. Disen-Forcing: Disentangling Semantic Anchoring from Motion for Autoregressive Video Diffusion

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Autoregressive video diffusion models (AR-VDMs) enable real-time, long-horizon video generation but degrade rapidly once the inference horizon outruns the training horizon, due to compounding exposure bias. To stabilize long rollouts, recent methods heuristically adopt frame sink, keeping initial frames as a persistent model context, inspired by attention sinks in LLMs. We revisit this design and find that the mechanism by which initial frames stabilize long-range consistency in AR-VDMs is not an LLM-style attention sink; instead, long-range consistency is anchored through a sparse subset of attention heads, while the majority of heads predominantly capture local temporal dynamics. We propose Disen-Forcing, a distillation-time plug-in for AR-VDMs that disentangles and reinforces this head specialization through disentangled context routing and motion-disentangling regularization. On long-horizon video generation benchmarks, Disen-Forcing simultaneously improves long-range consistency and motion dynamics of base AR-VDMs, a trade-off that sink-based methods fail to balance.

## 15. OmniOPD: Stable On-Policy Distillation for Joint Audio-Video Generation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.02803v1

- Authors: Zihao Fan, Xin Lu, Jie Huang, Hexin Zhang, Xueyang Fu, Zheng-Jun Zha
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Reinforcement learning (RL) elevates joint audio-video generation, but optimizing heterogeneous, modality-specific audio and video rewards often introduces cross-modal interference. On-policy distillation (OPD) alleviates this issue by transferring the capabilities of specialized audio and video RL teachers into a single student model. However, a significant imbalance in cross-modal token counts leaves audio with sparse supervision, causing unstable distillation. We observe that naively increasing the audio loss weight fails to solve this problem since audio learning relies heavily on visual context, indicating richer visual cues are crucial to offset sparse audio signals. To address this, we propose OmniOPD, a modality-wise on-policy distillation framework designed for balanced and stable audio-video generation. First, we construct privileged visual information using asymmetric noise scheduling, allowing the audio teacher to generate more informative targets from cleaner video context. Second, we introduce grouped multi-noise sampling to expand audio supervision coverage and stabilize training. Third, we introduce timestep-wise importance weighting based on teacher-student transition divergence, preserving reliable supervision while down-weighting unreliable learning signals, to stabilize fluctuations in audio learning. Evaluated on JavisBench and VBench demonstrate OmniOPD preserves audio quality while significantly enhancing video generation, achieving effective joint distillation.
