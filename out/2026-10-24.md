## 1. Progress Probes $P^2$: Lightweight Runtime Monitoring for Vision-Language-Action Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31613v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31613v1
- Authors: Atiksh Bhardwaj, Edward W. Duan, Junwon Seo, Prithwish Dan, Wei-Chiu Ma, Andrea Bajscy, Preston Culbertson
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models can perform diverse manipulation tasks directly from visual observations and natural language instructions, but they remain prone to mistakes and unpredictable failures. Deploying these policies without continuous human supervision will require robust runtime monitors that detect when the policy has gone off-course. Existing monitors use external foundation models, such as vision-language models (VLMs) to supervise the policy, introducing significant complexity and computational cost for monitoring at runtime. In this work, we investigate an alternate approach: \textit{can a VLA's own internal activations provide the information needed to monitor its execution?} Our method trains linear probes on policy activations from demonstrations to estimate progress, then flags executions that deviate from expected progress. In experiments with the open-source VLA $π_{0.5}$ and across simulated and real-world manipulation tasks, this approach is competitive with or outperforms VLM-based monitors in task failure detection, as well as methods which require failure data for training. These findings suggest that simple probes of VLA internals can be used to develop reliable runtime monitors, opening a path toward building policies that can act reliably and monitor their execution without external foundation models.

## 2. Ask About the Right Moment: Training-Free Object Identity Reasoning for Video-Language Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31471v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31471v1
- Authors: Di Yang, Xiaochang Li, Muyang Liu, Yufei Wang, Yanhai Xiong
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: video-language

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Many questions about a video concern one physical object. Where does the ball end up after the cups are shuffled? Which card is the queen? How often was the mug covered? Video-language models (Video-LMs) recognise such objects in single frames but do not keep them apart over time. On real shell-game videos, open models from 8B to 235B parameters, their thinking variants, a reinforcement-learned tool agent and three training-free tool agents all stay within ten points of chance. We separate semantic identity binding from temporal identity maintenance. An object's identity should be fixed at a moment when the evidence for it is visible, and carried afterwards by a tracker. \emph{Track, Then Bind} implements this principle without training. A promptable video tracker follows every candidate object and draws it with a track number. A frozen vision-language model answers a static question about the identifying evidence, which binds the target to a track. The answer is then derived from the tracked state in text, without asking the model to reinterpret the original video during read-out. A single configuration covers all benchmarks. With Qwen3-VL-32B it solves both VET-Bench tasks, where a fine-tuned trajectory model reaches 92\% and 90\% and the same VLM voting over 16 samples stays at 30--32\%. It answers 55.4\% of the hardest Perception Test cup games, where the best published model answers 41.5\%, and, using track-derived timelines, 48.6\% of TOC-Bench questions, against 37.1\% for the same model answering directly. Drawing the same tracks on the video and asking the question directly falls 12--19 points short of the agent on real cup games.

</details>

## 3. FastHarness: Experience-Driven Review Scheduling for Vision-Language-Action Policies

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Agentic robot systems combine language-model reasoning with robot policies to monitor execution and recover from failures. However, reviewing every action chunk incurs substantial overhead, even in familiar situations. We introduce FastHarness, an experience-driven execution harness that adaptively schedules GPT-6 Astra supervision of a π0.5 policy. A fast path uses stage memory and reviewed outcomes to reduce review frequency as experience accumulates, while a complementary recovery path focuses supervision on correcting errors. These results highlight adaptive supervision as a practical route to more efficient agentic robotics without additional model training.

## 5. Lightweight Frame Compensation for Real-Time Polyp Segmentation under Frame Skipping
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31228v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31228v1
- Authors: Weihao Gao
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Real-time polyp segmentation in computer-assisted colonoscopy is hampered by the difficulty of deploying high-accuracy models under strict latency budgets. Frame skipping, which performs inference every K frames and reuses or interpolates the rest, improves throughput but introduces motion blur and lesion misalignment that degrade temporal consistency. To close this gap, we propose the Lightweight Frame Compensator (LFC), a 77K-parameter plug-and-play module inserted before a frozen segmentation backbone. Operating on a three-frame window, LFC fuses consecutive frames via spatio-temporal attention into a single compensated frame, and is trained only on pseudo-labels generated by a differently seeded model, without any expert annotation. Experiments on three polyp video benchmarks show that LFC recovers most of the accuracy degraded by direct frame skipping and, in several settings, reaches the accuracy of frame-by-frame inference, while retaining a 2.05X--2.55X throughput gain. We further evaluate the temporal stability of three backbones spanning CNN, pyramid Transformer, and global-attention Transformer architectures, revealing a spectrum of sensitivity to frame skipping that arises from how locally or globally each architecture aggregates misaligned input. The compensation gain of LFC follows this spectrum: it is largest for high-capacity Transformer backbones, which achieve the best static accuracy but suffer the most under skip-frame inference. LFC thus offers a lightweight, pluggable, and deployment-friendly route to real-time polyp segmentation.

</details>

## 7. Where Do Long Captions Fail? Position-Aware Diagnosis and Reinforcement Learning for Detailed Image Captioning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31111v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31111v1
- Authors: Yuanze Hu, Zhichao Yang, Junwei Jing, Xin Yang, Wenxuan Liu, Tinghai Zhang, Jia Fu, Hongzhi Zhang...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Detailed image captioning is usually framed as an amount problem: a model should mention more visual facts while avoiding hallucination. However, autoregressive VLMs generate captions as ordered sequences, and later generations may be produced under weaker or noisier visual grounding. We therefore argue that long-form captioning should be studied not only by what facts are mentioned, but also by where they appear. We introduce a position-aware rubric representation that decomposes dense human references into atomic visual claims, verifies generated captions against those claims, and induces a quartile-wise composition of supported, hallucinated, and generic content. This lens reveals two qualitatively different failures in long captions: models tend to produce unsupported specificity in the middle, while the final portion often escapes into vague, non-evidential language rather than simply accumulating more hallucination. A same-reference human control does not exhibit these patterns; additional human audits examine the visual validity of the induced labels. Motivated by this diagnosis, we propose PAGER (Position-Aware Grounded Evidence Reinforcement), a GRPO training objective with separate signals for supported fine-grained middle evidence, evidence-bearing tail continuation, claim coverage, and content-aware length control. The result is a closed loop: the same ordered claim representation identifies where long captions fail and supplies the position-specific reward needed to repair those failures. Experiments on detailed-caption benchmarks show that PAGER improves position-wise grounding and pairwise caption preference while preserving competitive global caption quality.

</details>

## 11. HiP3R: Hierarchical Pose Graphs for Long-Horizon 3D Reconstruction
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.31635v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.31635v1
- Authors: Zunnan Xu, Shangchen Zhou, Yihang Luo, Chen Change Loy
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: streaming

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Feed-forward models accurately estimate camera motion and 3D geometry from short video windows. However, computational and memory costs prevent direct inference on sequences containing thousands of frames. Long videos must therefore be processed as overlapping chunks. This division produces independent local reconstructions with inconsistent coordinate frames and duplicate estimates of shared cameras. Achieving global consistency requires propagating constraints from adjacent overlaps and distant revisits across the sequence. This requirement naturally motivates pose graph optimization. However, the choice of graph level creates a trade-off. Chunk-level graphs efficiently resolve scale drift and close large loops. Yet, their rigid transformations cannot correct internal errors or conflicting frame predictions. Conversely, frame-level optimization offers local flexibility, but scaling it globally is computationally expensive and can break metric scale. HiP3R resolves this trade-off using a hierarchical pose graph. HiP3R first optimizes a robust chunk-level $\mathrm{Sim}(3)$ graph to establish the global layout and scale. It then holds this layout fixed and estimates one shared $\mathrm{SE}(3)$ camera per frame, turning overlapping window predictions into a single trajectory. Experiments across multiple benchmarks demonstrate the effectiveness of HiP3R in improving camera trajectories and 3D reconstruction. Our method applies to both chunk-based and streaming backends without additional training or fine-tuning. The codes are available at https://kkakkkka.github.io/HiP3R.

</details>
