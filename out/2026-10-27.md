## 1. SyncPro: Synchronous Reasoning and Responding for Proactive Streaming Video Understanding
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32132v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32132v1
- Authors: Xueyun Tian, Bingbing Xu, Jiaming Liu, Jinpeng Yu, Yang Shi, Yu Zhao, Ruihua Huang, Yuanzhuo Wang...
- Keyword score: 7 / hits: streaming, video understanding, reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Proactive streaming video understanding requires assistants to track unfolding events and respond when a user's requested condition is met. To make these decisions, assistants must accumulate relevant evidence and reassess response conditions as new observations arrive. Existing work offers complementary capabilities for this process: proactive video assistants learn when to respond, while streaming video reasoning methods generate scheduled intermediate text to organize observations and support subsequent answers. Together, these capabilities provide a foundation for proactive reasoning, but how to coordinate ongoing reasoning with timely responses remains underexplored. In a direct adaptation, fixed reasoning intervals can trigger updates when nothing relevant has changed or postpone useful updates until the next scheduled step. Sharing one generation path also couples two distinct requirements: preserving evidence for future decisions and producing only the response requested by the user. Moreover, responses generated after reasoning must wait for it to finish, even when sufficient evidence is already available. To address these challenges, we introduce SyncPro, a synchronous dual-stream framework with specialized generation paths for evidence tracking and user-facing responses. Its Inner and Response branches share causal history and activate independently, allowing relevant evidence changes to trigger internal updates and responses to proceed concurrently with ongoing reasoning. We construct 189K dual-stream examples and train SyncPro through supervised fine-tuning followed by trajectory-level reinforcement learning. Experiments across three proactive benchmarks show improved response timing and more accurate answers to user requests.

</details>

## 2. Video-MemComp: Taylor-Guided Sensitivity-Aware Memory Management for Streaming Video Understanding
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32784v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32784v1
- Authors: Yonghui Niu, Yuhao Ji, Chang Nie, Jiao Zhang, Yilong Li, Junlan Feng, Ran He, Caifeng Shan...
- Keyword score: 6 / hits: streaming, video understanding

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The rapid growth of video data has created an urgent need for efficient video understanding systems that can handle long and continuous visual inputs, posing significant challenges for Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) due to severe memory bottlenecks. Moreover, existing compression methods often overlook the explosive accumulation of approximation errors. To address this issue, we propose Video-MemComp, a training-free and sensitivity-aware context management framework for memory-constrained streaming video understanding. Our method theoretically decomposes compression error into orthogonal local approximation error and layer-wise structural sensitivity via a first-order Taylor expansion. To minimize local approximation error, we perform token aggregation in a pre-RoPE safe zone with an attention-conserving logit bias, mitigating geometric distortions caused by RoPE rotations. To manage layer-wise structural sensitivity, we dynamically allocate the KV cache budget based on offline gradient priors and online context entropy, prioritizing high-sensitivity layers. Video-MemComp outperforms or achieves performance comparable to full-cache and state-of-the-art compression methods on benchmarks including MLVU, VideoMME, and EgoSchema. Notably, it improves the overall accuracy of Qwen2.5-VL-3B from 62.3% to 64.1% while reducing the KV cache to an equivalent of 6,000 tokens per layer, saving over 90% of KV memory.

</details>

## 3. DiscoDemo: Discovering Efficient and Diverse Robot Demonstrations for Imitation Learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Behavior cloning for robotic manipulation is highly sensitive to the coverage of its training data, yet collecting diverse successful demonstrations remains costly. We study reinforcement learning (RL) not as a deployable controller, but as a data generator for downstream visuomotor imitation learning. Our key premise is that multiple successful solution modes cover a broader range of successful trajectories than repeatedly collecting a single narrow solution. We introduce DiscoDemo, which trains a privileged latent-conditioned RL generator with a parallel reverse curriculum and a success-gated diversity objective to discover diverse solutions while preserving task success. The trained generator produces multiple successful closed-loop behaviors, which are safety-filtered and exported as a static dataset for downstream imitation learning. Across four manipulation tasks, DiscoDemo produces broader successful-state coverage and improves downstream visuomotor policies in both simulation and the real world. Averaged across tasks, downstream success increases to 62% from 40% for the strongest baseline in simulation, and to 78% from 39% on the real robot, respectively. These results suggest that successful solution diversity improves imitation learning for a pretrained flow-matching VLA and remains complementary to corrective data collection. ## 5. DeformStruct: Deformable Structure Modeling for Point-Supervised Temporal Action Localization
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32321v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32321v1
- Authors: Mengnan Liu, Sanping Zhou, Kun Xia, Yonghao Dong, Jie Wang, Gang Hua
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: temporal action, temporal localization

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Point-supervised Temporal Action Localization (PTAL) aims to localize action instances using only a single annotated timestamp for each instance. Such sparse supervision offers limited guidance for modeling the structural organization of an action instance. Existing PTAL methods primarily infer action regions from classification responses, leaving the structural organization of multiple action-related parts within each instance insufficiently modeled. To address this problem, we propose DeformStruct, an instance-level deformable structure modeling framework for PTAL. DeformStruct progressively constructs the structure of each action instance through three stages. First, Structural Anchor Mining discovers multiple anchors from annotated points and action-responsive regions, providing structural nodes grounded in the current instance. Second, Deformable Part Alignment associates latent structural parts with these anchors through deformation-aware matching, allowing their temporal supports to adapt to instance-dependent variations. Third, Relational Structural Organization explicitly models the relative temporal relations among the aligned parts, forming a relational representation that characterizes how different action-related parts are organized together. Extensive experiments on THUMOS14, ActivityNet-v1.3, GTEA, and BEOID demonstrate consistent improvements in point-supervised temporal localization and validate the effectiveness of deformable structural modeling.

</details>

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

While wide-angle lenses provide an expanded field of view (FoV), they exhibit severe nonlinear distortion near image boundaries, unnaturally stretching people in these regions. To tackle these issues, we propose \textbf{FastVC}, an efficient full-body video rectification framework driven by distortion-aware spatiotemporal learning. At the model level, neighborhood spatiotemporal attention (NST) aggregates local context and selectively fuses historical features with low overhead. At the optimization level, sparse spatiotemporal supervision applies spatial alignment only to anchor frames, decoupling frame-wise accuracy from temporal stability. Extensive experiments show that FastVC achieves state-of-the-art rectification fidelity and visual stability at 57.0 FPS.

## 7. UniWorld: A Unified World Model for Real-Time Visual World Simulation

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Continuous visual world simulation requires coupling evolving scene geometry with temporally persistent appearance. We present UniWorld, a unified framework that integrates depth trajectories and reference appearance within a shared video generation model. The central design maintains visual and geometric histories on a common timeline. Noise-aligned overlapping continuation places generated history at the prescribed sampling noise level and commits only newly generated latents. The measured steady-state DiT execution path achieves real-time generation throughput, demonstrating the efficiency of UniWorld for continuous visual world simulation.

## 8. Retiming Robot Demonstrations with a Learned Speed Prior
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.32676v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.32676v1
- Authors: Seungyong Moon, Deokjae Lee, Hansang Lee, Munkhtselmeg Battsengel, Youngin Kim, Seonghyeon Yun, Sungjin Jeon, SongEun Kim...
- Keyword score: 2 / hits: real-time, vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The pace of a robot demonstration is often determined by the teleoperator rather than by the task itself. As a result, the same motion can appear at widely different speeds across demonstrations, and policies trained on such data may inherit operator-specific timing along with task behavior. We propose to learn a speed prior directly from large-scale demonstration data. Given a task instruction, a motion path and the images along it, it predicts a distribution over execution speed along the path, from which a chosen quantile defines a consistent clock for retiming each demonstration. Existing methods derive the acceleration signal from the very data they accelerate, so each new task costs another fit, annotation, or pair of predefined rates. A single trained speed prior instead covers every task in a corpus. We fit a speed prior on both robot teleoperation data and handheld demonstrations in which a human arm, rather than a robot arm, moves the gripper, and use it to retime robot demonstrations to the handheld pace before fine-tuning a vision-language-action policy. For execution, we pair the policy with a real-time optimal-control layer that tracks predicted action chunks under robot constraints using the predicted pace. On 120 RoboLab tasks, policies fine-tuned on our learned clocks succeed more often and move with up to half the jerk compared with policies fine-tuned on the original demonstration timing and uniformly accelerated to the same speed. On a real robot, our policy stacks cups in 3.50s, 1.72$\times$ faster than the default policy's 6.02s while succeeding as often, in 29 of 30 trials. Videos and further material are available at https://mllab.snu.ac.kr/projects/learned_speed_prior.

</details>

Flow-matching Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models generate expressive continuous trajectories for robotic manipulation, yet balancing real-time responsiveness with execution stability remains a persistent challenge. <details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Collecting high-quality demonstrations remains a central bottleneck for learning dexterous manipulation.
