## 1. PACE: Plan, Attend, Check, and Emit for Training-Free Response Timing in Streaming Video Understanding
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28323v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28323v1
- Authors: Siqi Guo, Yuxia Chen, Wenbo Zhu, Yangguang Ji, Zhenxiang Jiang, Teng Zhong, Yu Zhang
- Keyword score: 6 / hits: streaming, video understanding

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Streaming video understanding requires a model to decide not only what to answer but also when to answer. Evidence arrives progressively, and recent benchmarks identify premature responses, issued before the queried event has concluded, as a recurring failure mode. Existing approaches often learn response timing through additional training, while training-free alternatives typically rely on scalar gating signals that do not explicitly represent when an answer becomes final. We organize chunk-wise streaming errors into three patterns: sampling variation can make an answer unstable, a stable answer may lack support in the observed video, or an answer may be supported by the current evidence while the event remains unfinished. We propose PACE (Plan, Attend, Check, and Emit), a training-free framework designed around these three failure modes. Given a question, PACE drafts a plan specifying what to observe and what will signal completion. As the stream unfolds, it invokes an off-the-shelf video LLM at informative moments, evaluates each draft for stability, grounding in its cited frames, and completion, and records verified observations in an event ledger. Experiments on ProactiveVideoQA and OVO-Bench Forward show that PACE improves over the official protocols by up to 5.9 average PAUC points and 6.2 average accuracy points across proprietary and open-source backbones, yielding state-of-the-art performance.

</details>

## 2. FIT-MAC: A Closed-Loop Multi-Agent Framework for Robust Vision-Language-Action Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28109v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28109v1
- Authors: Jiatong Li, Jisheng Dang, Qiangqiang Ma, Bimei Wang, Jingwen Zhao, Wenjun Ma, Juncheng Li, Hong Peng...
- Keyword score: 6 / hits: vision-language-action, multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-language-action (VLA) models have achieved strong performance on standard robotic manipulation benchmarks. However, their reliability can degrade substantially when task conditions deviate from the nominal setting. Perturbations in object positions and task instructions may lead to complete task failure. Existing VLA policies generally lack an explicit runtime mechanism for distinguishing these failure modes and selecting an appropriate recovery strategy. To systematically mitigate this issue, we propose FIT-MAC, a failure-aware, closed-loop multi-agent control framework for improving the robustness of VLA policies at inference time. FIT-MAC augments a VLA policy with an inference-time control layer. First, the Planner Agent parses the language instruction into a structured task specification that captures the manipulation goal and spatial constraints, and the Grounder Agent resolves each instruction-level reference to a visual target. Next, the Verifier Agent tracks phase-specific execution progress, distinguishes target-binding errors from local execution failures, and determines whether intervention is warranted. The Correction Agent then routes permitted interventions to target rebinding or bounded grasping or placement recovery, depending on the failure type. When no intervention is required, the pretrained VLA remains the default action generator. Extensive experiments on LIBERO-PRO demonstrate that FIT-MAC improves robustness across multiple VLA backbones, consistently increasing success rates and achieving state-of-the-art performance under diverse perturbations.

</details>

## 3. StreamEval: Evaluating Interactive Streaming Video Generation Models

- Authors: StreamEval Team
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: streaming, real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

As streaming video generation advances, enabling real-time interaction with generated video is becoming increasingly important. We introduce StreamEval, a unified benchmark for interactive streaming video generation spanning five tasks: avatar dialogue, avatar performance, world generation, instruction editing, and reference editing. StreamEval comprises 300 scripted test cases lasting 15-300s, each specifying initial assets, incrementally arriving multimodal requests, and explicit requirements for state changes and content preservation. ## 4. Physical Canvas: A Vision Tactile World Model For Robot Policy Evaluation And Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28613v1

- Keyword score: 3 / hits: robot policy

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Evaluating and post-training robot policies through physical interaction is costly and difficult to scale, motivating action-conditioned world models as interactive simulators. However, visually plausible predictions can misrepresent contact dynamics, providing unreliable feedback for policy evaluation and learning. We introduce PhysCanvas, a state-consistent visuo-tactile world model for contact-rich robot manipulation. PhysCanvas unifies multi-view RGB, tactile images, marker displacement, task state, and actions within a unified vision space. All modalities share a video VAE and pretrained generative backbone, allowing optical touch to reuse visual and temporal priors. Motion images derived through forward kinematics and camera projection provide spatially aligned action conditioning, while an initial RGB anchor and updated reference observations guide sequential generation. Our consistency-aware post-training uses the frozen Scene Reward Model, Modality Reward Model, and State Reward Model to supervise scene preservation, visual-tactile compatibility, and agreement with real task outcomes. The resulting world model supports closed-loop policy rollouts for evaluation and provides imagined interactions and rewards for GRPO policy updates. Across four real-world manipulation tasks, when policies are rolled out in the world model, State Reward Model supervision increases the arithmetic mean of task-wise Pearson correlations between world-model and real-world success rates from 0.602 to 0.885. Policy post-training in the reward-guided world model further increases $π_{0.5}$'s mean real-world success rate from a 59.1% SFT baseline to 70.8%. These results demonstrate the value of unified multimodal modeling and consistency-aware post-training for robot policy evaluation and learning. ## 5. OverSeer: Multi-Agent World Models Beyond What Agents See
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28554v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28554v1
- Authors: Runjia Li, Kam Woh Ng, Minghao Chen, Junlin Han, Jianyuan Wang, Zhaochong An, Juan-Manuel Perez-Rua, Andrea Vedaldi...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Despite the success of video world models, extending them to multiple agents is difficult because it requires generating several agent-specific video streams that are consistent in time and between agents. To address this gap, we introduce OverSeer, a multi-agent world model that augments egocentric agent views with auxiliary camera views. We suggest that, by fixing the auxiliary views in space, they act as a proxy for a world-centric representation: Unlike the agents' views, which can change entirely due to a simple change in agent position, changes in the auxiliary views directly map to localized changes in the world state. We show that these fixed views substantially help the model to maintain consistency. We also show that, due to their redundant and semi-static nature, the auxiliary views can be efficiently compressed with a delta-VAE that encodes only the changes relative to a static reference image, reducing the number of tokens required to process them. Experiments in Minecraft and Doom demonstrate improved world and agent consistency, with qualitative results on real-world multi-camera captures indicating that the approach is not tied to game engines. Our compression analysis further shows that delta-VAE substantially reduces the computational overhead of auxiliary-view generation. We conclude that these fixed views provide a simple and efficient way to improve multi-agent world models.

</details>

## 6. DIVE: Benchmarking Full-Duplex Interaction with Visual Engagement in Continuous Streams

- Keyword score: 3 / hits: streaming, real-time, video understanding

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Recent advances in multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have substantially improved spoken interaction and video understanding. Existing online and streaming benchmarks primarily assess causal multimodal perception through timestamped question answering, whereas real-world interaction requires models to continuously identify task-relevant multimodal cues and decide whether, when, and how to respond. Response quality varies substantially across models and tasks, with adapting ongoing responses to newly arriving information remaining particularly challenging. We hope DIVE facilitates progress toward more natural and capable real-time multimodal interactive systems.

## 7. MAS-Guard: Unified Guardrails from Individual Agents to Multi-Agent LLM Systems
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28397v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28397v1
- Authors: Mohammad Kachuee, Phu Mon Htut, Sheng Li, Nikolaos Pappas, Miguel Ballesteros Martinez, Yassine Benajiba, Sujith Ravi, Dan Roth
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Agentic AI extends large language models (LLMs) from generating responses to planning and executing complex workflows, substantially expanding their utility while exposing applications to unsafe actions, information leakage, and adversarial manipulation. Multi-agent systems further expand these capabilities through specialization and collaboration, but distributed authority, heterogeneous roles, and opaque or potentially compromised participants make safeguarding even more challenging. Existing defenses address important individual threats but provide fragmented coverage across local execution, system-level interactions, and enforcement mechanisms. We introduce MAS-Guard, a unified framework that closes this gap for safeguarding single- and multi-agent AI applications containing both instrumentable and opaque agents. Within instrumentable agents, the proposed local Agent Guardrail (A-Guard) layer applies task-specific experts to assess agentic events. At the system level, developer-owned Agent Charters and a Control Plane combine deterministic structural checks of routes, agent capabilities, and message formats with semantic assessments of roles and message content. These complementary layers enable enforcement through a shared set of interventions: allow, reflection, confirmation, and block. Evaluated across six benchmarks covering diverse single- and multi-agent scenarios with three agent LLMs from different providers, MAS-Guard reduces the overall attack success rate by 71.5-86.3% relative to unguarded agents, while meeting the utility reference criterion on benign tasks.

## 9. FALCA: Falsification-Aware Reinforcement Learning with Hierarchical Credit Assignment for Long-Horizon Deep Search Agents
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28361v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28361v1

- Keyword score: 3 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-horizon deep search requires LLM agents to iteratively decompose complex information needs, gather evidence from external sources, and maintain a reliable research state under multiple constraints. A central challenge is that early mistakes can be amplified over the search trajectory: agents may anchor on an incorrect candidate, collect partially supporting evidence, overlook contradictions, and accumulate errors that misguide subsequent reasoning. We propose FALCA, a falsification-aware framework that formulates deep search as a propose-falsify process. FALCA adopts a collaborative Searcher-Falsifier architecture, where the Searcher proposes candidate answers or sub-answers, while independent Falsifiers actively seek counterevidence, check unmet constraints, identify conflicts, and produce concise falsification reports for research-state revision. To strengthen this loop, we introduce falsification-aware training, combining SFT warmup on falsification trajectories with agentic RL based on parallel rollouts and hierarchical credit assignment over research stages, state transitions, and actions. Experiments on five search benchmarks show that FALCA improves long-horizon deep search performance over backbone and harness ablations, while reducing confirmation bias and error accumulation through more effective falsification and research-state revision.

## 10. CountVideoBench: A Benchmark to Evaluate Prediction Biases on the Object Counting task by Video-Language Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28356v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28356v1
- Authors: Marc Chapus, Carlos Crispim-Junior, Véronique Eglin, Atilla Baskurt
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: video-language

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Object counting in videos remains a poorly characterized capability of multimodal large language models. Counting questions are included in many existing VideoQA benchmarks, but the ground-truth count distributions in these benchmarks are heavily skewed toward small values. Across the nine benchmarks we survey, the median ground-truth count is at most five, and the three most frequent values together account for between 45% and 93% of the videos. The resulting mean accuracy is then dominated by the most frequent counts; a value-dependent bias at a rare count contributes negligibly to the average and remains invisible. We introduce CountVideoBench, a benchmark of 2,600 synthetic videos covering 26 cardinalities in the interval [5, 50] (100 videos each), grouped into six clusters of consecutive counts that span the range while enabling near-neighbor comparisons. Each video contains a single class of static objects under a controlled camera motion, removing object motion and reducing recognition as confounds. We evaluate sixteen video-language models from nine architecture families in a zero-shot setting. No model exceeds 27.6% Exact Match. The dependence of accuracy on cardinality varies across models, ranging from monotonic decline to a non-monotonic peak-and-collapse pattern, which rules out increasing difficulty with cardinality as the dominant explanation of counting failure. Every model further concentrates its predictions on its own set of preferred integers, with accuracy peaks coinciding with these values and collapsing at adjacent counts. These findings highlight the need for balanced, diagnostic counting benchmarks to reliably assess this fundamental visual capability.

</details>

## 11. Reasoning as a Distribution: Generative Plan Latents for Vision-Language-Action Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.28166v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.28166v1
- Authors: Matteo Cercola, Katie Z Luo, Milan Ganai, John Alora, Simone Formentin, Marco Pavone
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) policies increasingly reason before acting, but existing reasoning models compress this intermediate decision into a single representation. When several plans are valid, this commitment hides alternatives that could otherwise be selected or revised. We introduce the Generative Plan Latent (GPL), which represents the reasoning state as a low-dimensional conditional distribution over plans, such as which object, grasp, or route to use. This makes the reasoning state addressable: without retraining the VLA, plans can be sampled (coverage), selected on request (steering), and reweighted by deployment outcomes (outcome adaptation). We test all three on SimplerEnv manipulation with $π_0$, and extend the analysis to RoboCasa, LIBERO, and a maze domain. Steering the plan raises the fraction of executions that follow the request from 0.48 to 0.94, while the same guidance on the native action distribution shows no reliable gain. Finally, we show that an explicit plan is most useful when it carries decision information not already determined by the observation.
