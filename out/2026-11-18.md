## 1. BLIP-Live: Balancing Timeliness & Firing Precision in Proactive Streaming Video Agents
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21553v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21553v1
- Authors: Kanchana Ranasinghe, Honglu Zhou, Dominick Reilly, Le Xue, Shusheng Yang, Juan Carlos Niebles, Silvio Savarese, Ran Xu
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: streaming, video understanding

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Streaming video understanding is becoming increasingly important for real-world interactive systems. Such systems must be proactive for natural and useful human interaction: they should respond as soon as sufficient evidence becomes available while avoiding premature, redundant, or incorrect responses. We present BLIP-Live, a framework unifying data, metrics, harness, and training around a shared formulation of proactivity as effective causal response decisions balancing the two competing objectives of responding vs silence. First, we construct BLIP-Live-780K dataset containing human annotations of proactive response content and timing, which defines this desired behavior. We then propose PAUC-F, a metric to effectively measure our desired proactive behavior. PAUC-F combines existing response timeliness metrics with firing precision to reward early useful responses without rewarding excessive firing. With our desired behavior defined and measurable, we finally investigate model-agnostic inference harnesses that draw out proactive streaming capabilities of offline vision language models (VLMs), together with training pipelines that learn to further improve their proactive responsiveness. Across both settings, we find that combinations of sliding visual windows, online interaction memory, and structured response outputs consistently yield strongest proactive performance. Our experiments result in two models, with our BLIP-Live-Qwen model achieving state-of-the-art performance across four proactive streaming benchmarks, with gains up to 44% over baselines and outperforming multiple closed API models. Our code and data will be released publicly.

</details>

## 3. ScaleVLA: Scaling Vision-Language-Action Models to 552 Billion Parameters

## 5. POKE: Probe Offline, Keep Efficiently for Vision-Language-Action Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21033v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21033v1
- Authors: Jaehyuk Lee, Yeajin Lee, Suhyun Bae, Donghun Lee
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Visual token pruning reduces the inference cost of Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models, but attention-based scores do not directly measure a token's effect on the predicted action. We define action sensitivity as the magnitude of the first-order action change when a token is replaced by its camera's mean token and estimate it for all tokens with a single backward pass. Computing it online is costly: scoring on the full (unpruned) graph needs an extra forward pass at every control step. Scoring on the pruned forward pass is cheaper but causes scoring-graph lock-in: removed tokens lose their direct paths through later layers and tend to receive lower scores, making those locations less likely to return. On replayed observations, even with a random conditioning mask, pruned-graph scoring re-selects 59% of the mask's tokens, versus 6% expected by chance; attention histories show a similar bias. POKE (Probe Offline, Keep Efficiently) computes full-graph action sensitivities offline and distills them into a student with under 0.3M parameters. At inference, the student scores the current observation without backpropagation or previous masks, and score-guided diversity selection retains the tokens. Keeping 32 of 512 visual tokens on LIBERO, POKE achieves 95.2% success on OpenVLA-OFT and 90.8% on $π_{0.5}$ at $1.85\times$ and $1.26\times$ speedups, outperforming attention-, diversity-, and distillation-based pruners, with larger margins on LIBERO-Plus.

</details>

## 6. FAVR: Flow-Aware Visual Residual Alignment for Vision-Language-Action Policies

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Pretrained video representations provide rich temporal information for robotic manipulation, yet effectively integrating them into existing vision-language-action (VLA) policies remains challenging. FAVR maintains a compact temporal visual state through linear Test-Time Training (TTT) and retrieves action-conditioned visual features throughout flow-based action generation. ## 7. Instructions Before Actions: Prospective and Retrospective Language Supervision for Driving Vision-Language-Action Data
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.20631v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.20631v1
- Authors: Angel Martinez-Sanchez, Ross Greer
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Driving vision-language-action (VLA) models can accept navigation instructions, yet predicting a driver's trajectory does not establish that a model follows the instruction. We study how driving-language supervision is collected and evaluated, distinguishing prospective instructions issued before an action from retrospective labels written after observing it. We develop ADVLAT-Engine to synchronize spoken instructions, road-scene video, and vehicle trajectories, and compare three navigation apps on 48 replayed routes. On 87 maneuvers announced by both Google Maps and a passenger, the passenger uses more scene-dependent language. We additionally collected retrospective annotations from four people for the driving recordings and describe controlled comparisons of annotation content, timing, and downstream learning; those analyses are pending. Our findings separate maneuver prediction from instruction-following and establish a framework for comparing prospective and retrospective supervision.

## 10. ViRA: Toward Ultra-High-Resolution Visual Agents via Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21605v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21605v1
- Authors: Huawei Lin, Jinting Liu, Tongyan Wang, Xuyang Chen, Ruihua Shi, Zhichao Xu, Jiaxin Qin, Guoshenghui Zhao...
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Visual agents answer questions about ultra-high-resolution photos by zooming into the relevant regions. However, existing agents often answer from common sense instead of reading the evidence. Reinforcement learning (RL) on the final answer cannot correct this, because a guess earns the same reward as reading. We propose Cross-Input Relative Policy Optimization (CrossRPO), which trains agents on contrast sets. A contrast set asks one question about several versions of an image that differ only in a small region with the evidence and have different answers, so only reading the evidence solves the whole set. CrossRPO achieves 63.3% set accuracy on ViRA-Bench at the maximum resolution, against 59.3% for GRPO. ViRA-9B is the most accurate open model of its size on all benchmarks, and ViRA-27B outperforms Claude Haiku 5.5 and GPT-6-Luna, both at their highest thinking level, on most benchmarks.

</details>

## 12. MovieRoll: A Systematic Framework for Rolling Out Long-Form Videos
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.21339v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.21339v1
- Authors: Yunyang Ge, Weijie Kong, Ryan Xu, Yue Wu, Qi Tian, Jiangfeng Xiong, Bin Lin, Mingzhe Zheng...
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: streaming

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Streaming video generation must sustain quality beyond the training horizon while handling self-generated history and growing cache costs. To address these challenges, we present MovieRoll, a base-to-distilled framework that connects streaming fine-tuning, long-rollout distillation, and long-horizon inference. We first establish a robust multi-step base through Window-wise Teacher Forcing (WinTF), which jointly denoises chunks at progressive noise levels with bidirectional attention within each window and causal access to preceding history. To extend generation beyond the training horizon, KV recomputation bounds context for both models, while guidance annealing (AnnealCFG) reduces guidance toward the clean endpoint to stabilize MovieRoll-Base. Complementing these modeling advances, FBCA attention and PS-VAE decoding support efficient training and streaming output. On HeliosBench, MovieRoll-Base remains competitive at 30s after training on videos of up to 15s, and MovieRoll-Distilled achieves the highest total scores among the compared methods across single- and multi-event evaluations up to 60s, combining sustained naturalness with active motion.

</details>
