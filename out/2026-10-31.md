## 1. SymAnt: Grounding Long-Horizon Action Anticipation with Adaptable Symbolic Priors
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40632v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40632v1
- Authors: Fnu Aryan, Yaqi Xie, Joseph Campbell, Woojun Kim, Kwonjoon Lee, Hossein Nourkhiz Mahjoub, Ehsan Moradi-Pari, Katia Sycara...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: action anticipation

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

As robots and intelligent systems increasingly interact with humans, they must anticipate long sequences of future actions from only brief observations. While purely neural models typically require extensive observations to predict future actions and remain difficult to interpret and adjust, symbolic priors offer a structured alternative. To this end, we introduce SymAnt, a novel neuro-symbolic approach that unifies the expressiveness of neural networks with the structured reasoning of scene and knowledge graphs. Given a short video, SymAnt performs a joint-search over both graphs to construct a symbolic context, enabling unified reasoning over spatial relationships, object attributes, affordances, and associated actions. This symbolic context serves as a prior for an iterative refinement process that progressively generates and improves the sequence of future actions. By grounding predictions in symbolic context, SymAnt anticipates long-horizon actions from short observation windows in a parameter-efficient manner, while enabling users to directly edit the underlying knowledge to adjust predictions without further training. Through our experiments, we demonstrate the effectiveness of SymAnt, particularly when only short observation contexts are available, across five diverse datasets: Breakfast, 50 Salads, EPIC-KITCHENS, EGTEA Gaze+, and Assembly101.

</details>

## 2. JEDI: Joint-Embedding Demonstration Compression for In-Context Imitation Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40356v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40356v1
- Authors: Jiazhao Liang, Zhewen He, Shuaihang Yuan, Hao Huang, Yi Fang
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: imitation learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In-context imitation learning (ICIL) adapts robot policies through test-time demonstrations, but long multimodal trajectories constrain how many examples fit in context. We ask what a compressed demonstration should preserve. We propose JEDI, which compresses demonstrations into latent slots trained to predict the query episode's next-stage keyframe in a frozen video-JEPA space. Task and process counterfactuals help the slots preserve execution details, while the predictor selects demonstrations from a bank. Post-training with keypoint tokens enables human-hand conditioning on robots. Experiments on LIBERO and real robots show that JEDI supports many-shot context; LIBERO objective controls show improved success and demonstration following over action-only compression. These findings support query-future prediction as a training objective for preserving execution-relevant information in compressed demonstrations.

</details>

## 3. Reinforcement Learning Under Runtime Confounding: Policy Learning and Off-Policy Evaluation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40056v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40056v1
- Authors: Raphael Kim, Michele Santacatterina, Ramin Zabih, Keith Barnatchez
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Reinforcement learning (RL) is increasingly used to train robust, adaptable systems across healthcare, e-commerce, and robotics. In these settings, legal, ethical, or logistical concerns may preclude certain key variables that drive decisions from being collected during deployment time. This gives rise to a setting known as runtime confounding: the logged data hold full observability, but deployment time lacks knowledge of certain variables, known as runtime confounders. Unlike single timepoint and non-adaptive settings, runtime confounding under reinforcement learning has the potential to propagate over time, as earlier decisions affect future states, observations, and action assignments, leading to biased learning. We address these problems with three contributions: we (1) develop policy learning algorithms under runtime-confounding, (2) develop semi-parametric efficient off-policy estimation procedures, and (3) provide diagnostics for understanding the price of runtime confounding.

</details>

## 4. RoboSeed: Multidimensional Evaluation of Vision-Language-Action and World-Action Models: Capability, Resource Affordability, and Research Evidence

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Choosing a robot action model requires evidence about task capability, adaptation cost, deployment requirements, and the reliability of reported conclusions. Success rates alone offer limited guidance to researchers with different computing budgets and goals. We propose RoboSeed, a multidimensional evaluation framework for vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action models (WAM), within a continuing program to train, evaluate, and openly release robot models with verifiable evidence for model selection. The framework defines five dimensions: capability, downstream training affordability, deployment affordability, locally verified research value, and reproduction evidence. We apply the framework to representative VLA and WAM models, drawing on local training, released-weight evaluation, task-level audits, and an evaluation-system study. The results reveal budget-dependent backbone choices, sensitivity to adaptation recipes, and differences between language and visual task interfaces. Reproducible scoring code and machine-readable evidence support extension to additional models. The study provides dimension-level assessments and priorities for further measurement, while incomplete serving traces and heterogeneous protocols preclude overall numerical rankings.

## 6. From Reasoning to Action: Consequence-Grounded Alignment for Vision-Language-Action Models

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Reasoning informs decision-making by connecting intended actions with their expected consequences. Recent vision-language-action (VLA) models incorporate embodied chains of thought (ECoTs) to guide action generation. However, such guidance remains implicit, as actions are conditioned on ECoTs rather than explicitly optimized to align with them. We introduce CoRAct, a consequence-grounded reasoning-to-action alignment framework that explicitly aligns reasoning and action through reward-guided optimization in action noise space. CoRAct formulates two physically grounded ECoT components as verifiable signals: movement specifies intended motion, while waypoints encode expected spatial consequences. A unified Mixture-of-Transformers jointly models ECoT reasoning, action generation, and action-conditioned future prediction. These rewards guide group-relative optimization of the initial noise distribution, steering action generation toward consistency with intended actions and expected consequences. Experiments across RoboCasa, VLABench, and real-world manipulation tasks demonstrate improvements in task success, generalization, and reasoning-execution alignment over strong ECoT-based VLA baselines. CoRAct also grounds externally provided ECoTs in executable behavior, enabling zero-shot adaptation to novel scenarios by leveraging knowledge from powerful vision-language models. ## 8. Hierarchical Step Decomposition for Training-free Online Video Step Grounding
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.40153v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.40153v1
- Authors: Long Zhou, Luca Zanella, Paolo Rota, Massimiliano Mancini, Elisa Ricci
- Keyword score: 2 / hits: temporal localization, streaming

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Given a set of procedural steps, Video Step Grounding (VSG) aims to identify which steps occur in a video and localize when they are performed. Existing methods are either offline, requiring access to the full video before making predictions, or online, but relying on fixed-length video segments producing noisy, over-segmented outputs. In this work, we propose Hierarchical Step Decomposition (HSD), a training-free framework for online VSG that leverages the semantic understanding capabilities of Large Multimodal Models. HSD addresses two central challenges in online grounding: constructing semantically coherent temporal segments from a streaming video, and bridging the semantic gap between coarse procedural step descriptions and visual observations. To construct elementary temporal units, HSD incrementally groups incoming segments into semantically consistent events, using an anchor caption that is continuously validated against the visual stream. Based on these events, we perform zero-shot procedural step classification and selectively refine uncertain predictions by decomposing candidate steps into finegrained substeps that capture more nuanced visual evidence. By operating on semantically coherent events rather than every fixed-length segment, HSD reduces redundant computation and produces more stable predictions. Experiments on HT-Step, Ego4D Goal-Step, and CrossTask show that HSD substantially outperforms state-of-the-art methods, overall achieving better step recognition, more accurate temporal localization, and faster inference.

</details>

- What: 大規模マルチモーダルモデルを活用した、トレーニング不要なオンライン動画ステップグラウンディング手法「HSD」を提案。ストリーミング動画から意味的に一貫した時間セグメントを構築し、粗いステップ記述と視覚情報のギャップを埋める。
