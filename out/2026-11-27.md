## 1. You Only Look Multiple Times: YoloMT, a Real-Time Streaming Model on a T4
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34601v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34601v1
- Authors: Nessim Ben Abbes, Duc Han Le, Sabri Mtibaa, Van-Tam Nguyen
- Keyword score: 6 / hits: streaming, real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We introduce a new model for real-time streaming captioning on a single GPU. YoloMT is a 449.8M-parameter streaming captioner that provides Pareto-frontier performance among generative vision models in its class. Distilled from a 27B teacher by SFT and GRPO, it leads every model under 1B parameters on OVO-Bench, MVBench, StreamingBench and Video-MME, answered from its captions, and beats LFM2.5-VL-1.6B and SmolVLM2-2.2B. This allows the model to incorporate and infer from past frames without additional vision passes, making it efficient and effective. We also provide a detailed recipe for training YoloMT, with its stages and data, as well as ablations that demonstrate the contribution of each component.

## 2. LuxAvatar: Real-Time Relightable Head Avatars via Causal Neural Shading of 3D Intrinsic Proxies
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34940v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34940v1
- Authors: Yipengjing Sun, Chenyang Wang, Boyao Zhou, Jiapeng Zhu, Ka Leong Cheng, Shengping Zhang
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: streaming, real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

We present LuxAvatar, a feed-forward framework for real-time head avatar animation and relighting from a single portrait. To achieve both 3D controllability and complex facial light transport, we factorize avatar synthesis into an animatable 3D intrinsic Gaussian proxy and a causal autoregressive neural shader. This proxy disentangles canonical geometry and reflectance attributes, rapidly rasterizing deformed Gaussians into view- and motion-consistent PBR buffers. Conditioned on these buffers, our neural shader formulates forward rendering as a conditional video diffusion process, bypassing explicit PBR computation to synthesize realistic shading effects under target illumination. We achieve real-time streaming through heterogeneous distillation from a pretrained multi-step bidirectional relighting teacher to a two-step causal autoregressive neural shader, inheriting broad synthetic lighting priors while minimizing conditioning and decoding latency. To further specialize these priors to human heads, we construct a hybrid relighting dataset for domain-specific fine-tuning, capturing authentic facial dynamics and physically grounded light transport. Extensive experiments show that our framework enables 3D-controllable, real-time portrait animation and relighting, outperforming state-of-the-art methods in visual fidelity, motion control, and lighting accuracy.

</details>

## 3. ShowNow: Fine-Grained Speculative Streaming for Responsive World Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.34693v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.34693v1
- Authors: Yiren Song, Guian Fang, Mike Zheng Shou
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: streaming, real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

ShowNow turns playback-time computation into responsive visual interaction. Project: https://github.com/showlab/ShowNow.

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

## 7. RePace: Reasoning about Demonstration Speedup for Faster Vision-Language-Action Policies

- Authors: Insup Lee, Hojin Jeon, Sunguk Shin, Seoungbin Bae, Taekwan Kim, Jinwoo Shin, Dongsu Han
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Since vision-language-action (VLA) policies can inherit the slow pace of human demonstrations, accelerating these demonstrations before fine-tuning can lead to faster VLA policies without an additional speed-selection module at deployment. Several offline speedup methods have focused on identifying segments that require less precision and assigning predefined speedup factors to the segments. However, even within a single segment, a speedup factor suitable for steady motion may be too high when precise alignment is required. To this end, this paper presents RePace, an offline reasoning framework that determines how much to accelerate each part of a demonstration. Given the task instruction, a pretrained language model uses the visual context and original motion of successive windows to interpret these candidate retiming effects and select a speedup profile for the complete episode. Experimental results demonstrate that RePace outperforms the baselines in overall task execution efficiency on both LIBERO and RoboCasa-Kitchen. ## 11. Commit2Drive: Perception-Grounded Language Decisions as Commitments for Trajectory Planning in Vision-Language-Action Driving Models

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models introduce language reasoning into end-to-end autonomous driving. They output a language decision together with a trajectory, which makes the planner more interpretable. However, in existing methods the language decision is either an independent output or an index that selects among similar candidate trajectories, and its annotations are not verified against the trajectory. To address this, we propose Commit2Drive, a framework that imposes the language decision as a consistency constraint on trajectory planning. On Bench2Drive, Commit2Drive reaches a Driving Score (DS) of 78.50 and a Success Rate (SR) of 55.91% with a lightweight model, which is competitive with the state of the art, and greatly reduces the language-action inconsistency.
