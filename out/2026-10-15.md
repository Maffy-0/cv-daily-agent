## 1. CALM: Decentralized Control of Cooperative Aerial Manipulation with Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18425v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18425v1
- Authors: Jiaxi Huo, Petre Ricioppo, Fausto Francesco Lizzio
- Keyword score: 7 / hits: reinforcement learning, multi-agent, marl

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Cooperative aerial manipulation is difficult under wind, payload motion, and noisy goal broadcasts because multiple Unmanned Aerial Vehicles (UAVs) are coupled through a shared payload. We present CALM (Cooperative Aerial Learned Manipulation), a decentralized controller for aerial manipulators carrying a basket while tracking a moving ground robot. CALM combines a parameter-shared Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) policy, trained using Multi-Agent Proximal Policy Optimization (MAPPO), with goal estimation, per-UAV Extended State Observers (ESOs), basket-mass estimation, and anticipatory formation-yaw feedforward. The anticipatory formation-yaw feedforward uses estimated goal motion to rotate the formation before position error develops, reducing basket-yaw lag without adding net commanded force. A disturbance-conditioned critic supports centralized training while execution remains decentralized. Across held-out trajectories, CALM achieves lower mean position and orientation errors than all evaluated baselines, including the matched arm baseline (7.68 versus 10.95 cm and 3.3 versus 4.7$^\circ$), the cable-suspended realization, and two model-based controllers. Under unseen full-information-path degradation, CALM remains statistically comparable or superior to the arm baseline. Videos are available at https://petreric.github.io/CALM/.

</details>

## 2. Meta-Video: Efficient Standardized Evaluation for Video Understanding
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18535v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18535v1
- Authors: Soumya Shamarao Jahagirdar, Anna Kukleva, Vishaal Udandarao, Samuel Thomas, Brian Kingsbury, Rogerio Feris, James R. Glass. Ameya Prabhu, Hilde Kuehne
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: video understanding, video-language

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Video understanding benchmarks are the primary yardstick for measuring progress in video-language modeling, and a growing body of work relies on them to claim improvements. The reported benchmark scores in these works, however, are almost always produced by frameworks (e.g., lmms eval) used as black boxes: certain evaluation choices like frame sampling, preprocessing, and input formatting are inherited from preset defaults and seldom inspected carefully. We show these unexamined choices are a major source of variance. Even for the same model checkpoint, evaluation configuration alone can shift accuracy by up to 19.5pp. Hence, it is unclear whether reported gains reflect genuine improvements in capability or differences in evaluation setup. Moreover, many benchmark samples offer little evidence of visual capability, since they can be answered without the video, and the full suites are expensive to evaluate. We address these issues in two parts. First, we curate existing suites for discriminability and efficiency, producing Meta-Video, a balanced 1,000-question benchmark over 791 videos spanning diverse tasks and durations, which largely preserves model rankings, at 5 times inference speedup for a full-frame count sweep experiment (4-128 frames). Second, we establish a standardized evaluation protocol by sweeping the configuration space to isolate choices that measurably affect results, distill them into best practices and reporting standards, and release reproducible reference scores across five models and seven benchmarks. We hope that the proposed evaluation framework and the Meta-Video benchmark will support video understanding research by providing a reproducible and efficient foundation to measure progress in a reliable way.

</details>

## 3. LIBERO-RIG: A Hardware-Setup Robustness Benchmark for Vision-Language-Action Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18843v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18843v1
- Authors: Andrea Yaoyun Cui, Hanyu Wang, Xinyi Wang, Bangzheng Li, Lin Zhang, Zhenggang Tang, Shyamal Buch, Dejia Xu...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Robot policies are expected to generalize across deployment environments, including variations in the robot itself: cameras are bumped and remounted, calibrations drift, controllers run at different rates, and end-effector pads wear down. We present LIBERO-RIG, a benchmark that perturbs only the robot's hardware and setup while holding every LIBERO task fixed. The benchmark spans eleven perturbation axes, including camera mounting and optics, camera-to-arm calibration, actuation gain, control rate, proprioceptive noise, base pose, table height, and gripper pads. Each axis has five severity levels, yielding 210 conditions across the four LIBERO suites. We evaluate nine recent vision-language-action models using 500 episodes per condition and substantiate each finding with rigorous statistical tests. Pooled success falls from 0.932 to 0.767 at the middle severity and 0.509 at the highest severity. Performance degradation varies substantially across perturbation axes and action interfaces, and training on visual perturbations does not mitigate faults that leave camera images unchanged. We release the benchmark together with manifests specifying the fixed perturbation parameters for each episode.

</details>

## 4. ForkCast: Predictive Recovery for Vision-Language-Action Policies via Counterfactual Futures
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18650v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18650v1
- Authors: Chenru Wen, Huajian Zeng, Haokai Ding, Jian Kang, Anqing Duan, Dawei Zhou, Dezhen Song, Xingxing Zuo
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies perform manipulation tasks using camera images and language instructions, but object slip or visual occlusion can disrupt execution. Recovery is typically learned during policy training or delegated to a monitor that triggers a scripted repair after detecting a failure. We introduce ForkCast, a predictive recovery framework that compares counterfactual futures to determine whether and how to intervene in a frozen VLA policy. At each decision point, ForkCast evaluates three candidates: executing the policy's nominal action chunk (Commit), adjusting the camera viewpoint (Reobserve), and replanning the arm trajectory (Replan). A candidate-conditioned world model forecasts the latent outcome of each candidate at a common future horizon, and a shared task-progress head scores the predicted outcomes. By evaluating the policy's own continuation alongside the repair candidates, a single comparison based on task progress determines both whether and how to intervene, without a failure detector, an intervention threshold, or updates to the VLA backbone. On five simulated RoboTwin 2.0 tasks with injected occlusion and object slip, ForkCast increases pooled success from 13.3% for the frozen $π_{0.5}$ policy to 30.0% and transfers to two additional VLA backbones without updating ForkCast. After adaptation on real-robot candidate rollouts, ForkCast also improves success over the base policy under both clean and visibility-limited conditions. Code will be released to benefit the research community.

</details>

## 5. VideoJev: System One Model for Video Understanding and Interaction
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18615v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18615v1
- Authors: Yuhan Zhu, Xiangyu Zeng, Qingyi Si, Chenxu Yang, Yifei Li, Luozheng Qin, Junhao Zhou, Dingyu Yao...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: video understanding

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Visual agents must turn video observations into timely decisions to track progress and choose what to do next. Building on the emerging System One paradigm, we introduce VideoJev, a family of models that cast video understanding and interaction as structured decision tasks. We adapt pretrained multimodal large language models (MLLMs) by replacing autoregressive answer decoding with a shared head that scores temporal intervals and textual options against video evidence. These results offer a unified, reusable decision interface for composing structured visual judgments into complex, open-ended workflows.

## 6. ACCRUE: Continual Off-Policy Reinforcement Learning for Robot Foundation Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18596v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18596v1
- Authors: Changyeon Kim, Kimin Lee, Yashraj Narang, Yijie Guo
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Reinforcement learning (RL) post-training improves robot foundation models (RFMs) beyond their demonstrations, but real-world deployment demands more: learning new tasks efficiently while retaining previously acquired behaviors. Existing continual RL methods on top of VLAs mitigate forgetting but rely on on-policy updates with limited forward transfer, whereas off-policy methods improve single-task sample efficiency without addressing learner persistence across tasks. We propose ACCRUE, a continual off-policy RL framework that carries lightweight actor and critic networks across tasks, allowing each stage to reuse behavioral and value knowledge acquired through prior RL. Across contact-rich assembly sequences in simulation and on real-robot hardware, ACCRUE accelerates subsequent-task learning with near-zero forgetting and significantly reduces real-robot training time by 31.0\% to 68.5\%.

## 8. Closing the Estimation Gap: Training-Free Transductive Inference for Cross-Domain Few-Shot Action Recognition
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18209v1

- Keyword score: 3 / hits: action recognition

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Cross-domain few-shot action recognition (CD-FSAR) aims to recognise unseen action classes in a target domain from a handful of labelled videos. Despite advances in representation learning, CD-FSAR inference still relies on prototypes estimated from extremely limited support examples. To assess this bottleneck, we perform an oracle analysis with the representation fixed. Constructing oracle prototypes and decision boundaries from ground-truth query labels improves 5-way 1-shot accuracy by 7--20 percentage points across five target datasets. Across five target datasets (HMDB51, UCF101, SSv2, Diving48, and RareAct) in both 1-shot and 5-shot settings, TRACE consistently improves a strong CD-FSAR baseline, including gains of more than six percentage points on HMDB51 1-shot, while recovering much of the oracle-estimated gap. ## 9. RESTA: Time-Scaling Augmentation for Data-Efficient Robot Imitation Learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Robot manipulation policies trained through imitation learning commonly rely on costly demonstrations recorded in one task direction and at one temporal resolution. ## 12. ChunkAct: Action Chunking for Agentic Robotics
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.18874v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.18874v1
- Authors: Bingxin Xu, Wenrui Bao, Xinxin Liu, Jiawei Zhou, Yuzhang Shang, Emilio Ferrara
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Agentic robotics places a large language model (LLM) agent in the robot control loop, where it composes perception, motion, and vision-language-action (VLA) tools under the guidance of a memory and thereby generalizes to new tasks without retraining. This generality comes at a steep price in efficiency. Every primitive requires a round of LLM reasoning over the full interaction history, so the cost of an episode grows with the number of primitives. We find this reasoning largely redundant: once a sub-goal is chosen, its primitives follow a routine already prescribed by memory. We present ChunkAct, which introduces action chunking to agentic robotics. Offline, ChunkAct compiles the memory into these chunks, so execution is memory-free at test time and the planner is reserved for decisions that require reasoning. LLM computation therefore scales with the number of decisions rather than the number of primitives. On LIBERO-Pro, ChunkAct improves task success by 5.0 points over the state of the art in agentic robotics while requiring 2.9x fewer LLM calls, 4.4x fewer input tokens, and 2.3x fewer output tokens.
