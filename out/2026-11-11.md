## 1. Distilling Instructions into Weights: Closed-Form Edits Fix a Robot Policy's Misreading
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.11894v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.11894v1
- Authors: Ali-Adeeb Abbas, Esmaeil Seraj
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: vision-language-action, robot policy

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Generalist robot policies often fail at one step of a task they can otherwise perform. We ask whether such a failure can be repaired by editing the policy's weights directly, without gradients, training data, or a change to the instruction. Told to put the cube then the banana in the bowl, a zero-shot $π_{0.5}$ vision-language-action policy in RoboLab releases the cube before it reaches the bowl. Replaying 21 of these decisions under rewordings shows that it binds "in the bowl" to the wrong object. Closed-form instruction distillation copies the per-layer effect of an explicit rewording into the language model's MLP weights. On 128 held-out seeds, success rises from 27 to 106 episodes, with 81 fixed and 2 broken ($p=7\times10^{-22}$), while an update of the same size in random directions leaves success unchanged. The edit also lifts two other tasks without detectable harm on the remaining 13, and a variant that stores 0.16% of the parameter count keeps most of its effect. Placed in the action expert or action head instead, the same distillation helps less or not at all. Our released tool reproduced the fix end to end on new held-out seeds, from 8/64 to 36/64. Edits aimed at perception, a missing grasp, contact physics, or the gripper threshold did not help, and of seven blind Claude Opus editors, only the one given the misread instruction produced an edit that passed held-out scoring. These results suggest that weight edits work best when a known-good input already exists to distil. Because reruns with unchanged weights flip about a third of paired outcomes, evaluations should pair seeds, rerun the unedited policy, and confirm gains on held-out seeds.

</details>

## 2. VLA Adaptation with Real and Simulated Data: A Case Study on Robotic Chess
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12676v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12676v1
- Authors: Matěj Kripner, Milan Straka
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action, reinforcement learning, behavior cloning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-language-action (VLA) models are trained as general-purpose foundation models for robot learning, and require fine-tuning for each downstream task and robot. This raises practical questions such as how much data and training updates are needed, how checkpoints should be selected, and whether simulation can be leveraged to alleviate the cost of real data collection. We study these questions on a task that requires both precise manipulation and spatial understanding of the scene. In this task, an SO-101 arm moves a chess king between two squares that are given only by their names, such as e4 and a5. We fine-tune $π_{0.5}$ with various dataset sizes and update budgets, using teleoperated demonstrations as well as simulated demonstrations from a randomized scripted expert that imitates the variability of human demonstrations. In simulation, the success rate scales with both data and updates. Importantly, we find that a short learning-rate cool-down adds up to 31 points and is necessary for comparing checkpoints, and that validation loss and action error are unreliable for model selection. On the real arm, we fine-tune on up to 256 teleoperated demonstrations and evaluate ten policies in 25 trials each. Partial results indicate that pretraining on the simulated demonstrations helps substantially, most clearly when real data is scarce. We also present a negative result, in which training on the policy's own rollouts with filtered behavior cloning, RECAP-style advantage conditioning, corrections from an automatic coach, or residual reinforcement learning did not yield a measurable improvement over plain behavior cloning in our setting. We release the simulator, the expert and the real demonstrations.

## 3. VLA-MOPD: On-Policy Distillation for Multi-Task VLA Policy Consolidation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12286v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12286v1
- Authors: Yongxuan Lv, Zhen Fang, Lichen Man, Hao Li, Pu Li, Hengyi Wang, Langfeng Cao, Lin Chen...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action, embodied agent, reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Physical intelligence requires embodied agents to ground language in observations and execute actions while retaining useful skills. Vision-language-action (VLA) policies offer a shared interface, but task-specific post-training often produces separate specialists. Consolidation is difficult: mixed-task reinforcement learning provides sparse trajectory-level credit, while offline distillation may miss student-visited observations and endpoint distillation does not supervise intermediate action-generation states. We introduce VLA Multi-teacher On-Policy Distillation (VLA-MOPD), a protocol for consolidating flow-matching VLA specialists. After acquiring task or suite experts, a shared student collects robot rollouts and queries the instruction-routed frozen teacher at each visited observation and intermediate ordinary differential equation (ODE) state. Starting from one trajectory per LIBERO task, VLA-MOPD raises mean success-once from 75.4% to 98.9%, compared with 98.15% for endpoint action distillation. It improves mean RoboTwin 2.0 success from 73.0% to 89.3%; on a Piper robot, two consolidation rounds improve average success from 70.0% to 90.0%. In a near-matched Spatial-adaptation study, reference matching limits the Object suite decrease to 3.0 points, versus 13.2 without the reference term.

## 4. HIST3R: Decomposing and Rectifying State Updates with History for Streaming 3D Reconstruction
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12011v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12011v1
- Authors: Kenneth Yang, Yu-Chiang Frank Wang
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: streaming

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Streaming 3D reconstruction with recurrent states enables long-video scene understanding under bounded memory, but turns reconstruction into a state-evolution problem: each incoming frame updates a compact scene memory on which all subsequent predictions depend, so unreliable updates overwrite historical context and accumulate into long-horizon geometric drift. We analyze CUT3R-style recurrent updates through a direction--magnitude decomposition, in which the magnitude sets the update gain, i.e., how strongly an observation is written into the state, and the direction encodes the geometric intent, i.e., where the update drives the state. This decomposition provides a unified view of CUT3R and its variants, revealing that existing training-free extensions perform magnitude-only control: they can attenuate an unreliable update but cannot correct its direction. We propose HIST3R, a training-free framework built on CUT3R that controls both components using history. HIST3R rectifies the update direction using locally consistent historical states, and modulates the update magnitude according to the visual discrepancy between the current observation and its history, suppressing redundant updates while preserving complementary evidence. Across camera pose estimation, 3D reconstruction, and video depth estimation, HIST3R consistently improves long-horizon performance while retaining the memory footprint and inference speed of CUT3R. With 1000 input frames, it reduces ATE by 23.87% on ScanNet and 33.65% on TUM-Dynamics relative to the strongest competing method; with 500 frames, it improves median reconstruction accuracy over the strongest CUT3R-based variant by 56.52% on 7-Scenes and 33.96% on NRGBD.

## 7. Reframe: Observation-Aligned Multi-Teacher On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Visual Reasoning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Agentic reinforcement learning (RL) and multi-teacher on-policy distillation (MOPD) offer complementary routes to post-training. For visual agents, however, tool use presents two related challenges. ## 9. Agentic-OPD: Recovering Forgotten Agent Capabilities via On-Policy Distillation for Agentic Tasks
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.12799v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.12799v1
- Authors: Tingcheng Bian, Kangye Gong, Jinchang Luo, MingQuan Cheng, Feiran Liu, Ni Li, Xiaoling Xia, Haiwei Wang...
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Reinforcement learning is now the mainstream paradigm for training LLMs on agentic tasks, yet standard practice keeps only the best-validation checkpoint and discards the rest. We show that this is lossy: in GRPO training of LLM agents, the best-validation checkpoint fails on problems that earlier checkpoints from the same run had already solved, and the union of problems solved across checkpoints far exceeds any single one. The discarded checkpoints thus form a teacher pool obtained at no additional training cost, which on-policy distillation (OPD) can mine to recover the lost coverage. Mining this pool raises two challenges: i) which checkpoint to distill from, as no checkpoint dominates the others and complementarity to the student is problem-specific; ii) where to trust it, as even a well-chosen teacher is only locally superior, so indiscriminate distillation overwrites behavior the student has already mastered. We propose Agentic-OPD, which treats the best-validation checkpoint as the student and all saved checkpoints as frozen teacher candidates, using the student's evolving capability gaps to guide both teacher selection and supervision filtering: the HCN policy (Highest Coverage Next) dynamically selects teachers by weighted coverage of the student's unsolved problems, while the LAD module (Locality-Aware Distillation) confines transfer to failed student rollouts on problems where the teacher performs better. Across five agentic task domains and 24 benchmarks and task subsets, Agentic-OPD improves over its initialization checkpoint by up to 7.4 points, with a 4B student surpassing the latest frontier models GPT-5.6 and DeepSeek-V4.1 on deep search. Agentic-OPD recasts a finished RL run as a reusable pool of policies and establishes post-RL capability recovery as a stage orthogonal to improvements in the RL algorithm itself.

</details>
