## 1. Residual Guidance for Real-Time Interactive Video Generation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32820v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32820v1
- Authors: NaHyeon Park, Kyeong-Hwan Moon, Youjin Jeon, Hyunjung Shim
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: streaming, real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Real-time interactive video generation allows users to change the prompt during generation to steer the evolving video. However, existing streaming models, which generate videos chunk by chunk, often keep following the previous prompt after a switch. We trace this failure to context inertia, where the previous chunk acts as a strong prior that suppresses the new prompt. Furthermore, we find that this inertia persists even when the cache retains only the previous chunk, yet discarding the context breaks visual continuity, which limits approaches that edit the cache. Rather than editing the cache, we propose Residual Guidance (RG), a simple, training-free method that counteracts context inertia with a correction measured outside the cache. Specifically, RG denoises the re-noised previous chunk once under the new prompt without the cache and adds the resulting residual to the early denoising steps of the next chunk, where its content is largely decided and the new prompt is most suppressed. Across settings ranging from a single switch to long multi-switch generation, RG substantially improves prompt switching capability on existing streaming models, including those with dedicated switching mechanisms, while largely retaining visual quality. As it requires no training or per-model tuning and adds only one forward pass per chunk, RG can be easily integrated into existing real-time video generation pipelines.

</details>

## 2. ReFocus: Progress-Guided Adaptive Practice for Long-Horizon Robotic Manipulation Reinforcement Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32639v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32639v1
- Authors: Haoyuan Li, Wei Huang, Jiehong Lin, Shengrong Zhang, Chenzhi Liu, Shengchao Zhou, Hehe Fan, Xiaojuan Qi...
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: vision-language-action, reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Online reinforcement learning (RL) enables vision-language-action policies to improve through environmental interaction. However, in long-horizon manipulation, sparse success/failure feedback provides little information about where policies encounter bottlenecks. Moreover, repeated full-task rollouts also leave these bottlenecks weakly analyzed and insufficiently practiced. To this end, we introduce ReFocus, a progress-guided multi-round RL framework that alternates between Global and Local RL to identify task-specific bottlenecks and allocate additional practice to them. Specifically, during Global RL, we decompose each task into a Directed Acyclic Graph (DAG) of subtasks. Then a DAG-based Progress Reward is leveraged for both policy training and bottleneck diagnosis. Based on the identified bottlenecks, ReFocus selects Adaptive Practice Windows, defining task segments from an entry stage to a target stage. It allocates additional interaction to these segments during Local RL while updating the entire policy. After that, it evaluates the policy to perform the full task for the next round of RL, forming an iteration between Global and Local RL. Experiments on LIBERO-Long and RoboCasa365 show that ReFocus achieves success rates of 97.8% and 51.3%, respectively, compared with 90.6% and 39.3% for the $π_{0.5}$ baseline. Furthermore, we evaluate a real-world adaptation on three long-horizon tasks. On BreadBaker, ReFocus achieves 95% success, compared with 30% for the $π_{0.5}$. Project page: https://refocus-robotics.github.io.

</details>

## 3. TESS: Query-Guided Temporal Event-Structured Frame Selection for Long-Video Understanding
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32410v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32410v1
- Authors: Qi Lu, Shiyan Tong, Shujie Li, Sen Li, Likun shi, Maofei Que, Xiaolong Jiang, Yao Hu
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: temporal localization, video understanding

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Temporal localization in long videos is challenging because brief, question-relevant events are dispersed among extensive redundant content. Limited visual budgets increase the risk of omitting these events or their temporal context, undermining reliable reasoning. Existing frame selection methods emphasize semantic relevance or temporal coverage, often overlooking the complementary evidence needed to locate related events and establish their temporal relationships. To address this limitation, we propose TESS, a training-free framework for query-guided temporal event-structured frame selection. TESS selects frames that jointly show the reference event and the event asked about, in the required temporal order. Leveraging frozen vision-language representations, TESS integrates soft temporal gating, role-aware budget allocation, and within-role redundancy suppression to preserve complementary event evidence. Extensive experiments across four long-video understanding benchmarks and three multimodal backbones demonstrate that TESS consistently outperforms the strongest compared baseline by 4.0-5.5 percentage points under a fixed frame budget. Further analyses reveal greater improvements on longer videos and robust performance under tighter visual budgets, while independent evidence annotations confirm enhanced event and temporal-relation coverage. These findings establish explicit temporal evidence organization as an effective training-free strategy for preserving event relationships and supporting efficient temporal reasoning over long videos. Our code is available at https://anonymous.4open.science/r/TESS_code.

</details>

## 4. Lazy Agents Answer Against Their Own Beliefs: Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Beliefs Teaches Them to Act on What They Believe
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.32884v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.32884v1
- Authors: Richmond Sin Jing Xuan, Peilin Feng, Wenbin Wang, Soujanya Poria
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

AI agents working on long tasks often commit to an answer before they have enough evidence, a behavior called laziness. In our evaluations, they frequently commit even while their stated belief still names several possible answers. An outcome reward at the end of the episode scores only the final answer, so it cannot teach the agent when to commit, because a lucky guess receives the same reward as an answer backed by evidence. During training, however, the simulator can compute the exact posterior over the answer at every step and tell whether the evidence is already enough. We propose Reinforcement Learning with Verifiable Beliefs (RLVB), which rewards the agent for taking further steps while this posterior still allows several answers and for committing once the evidence is sufficient. The reward needs no judge, critic or reward model, and it never reads the belief line the agent writes before each action. We also introduce Pro Worlds, a benchmark of visual puzzles where the agent views a large world through a small window. We evaluate three vision-language models on three benchmarks, two of which are never seen in training. RLVB improves the base model in all nine settings, by 17.1% on average. Under the outcome reward, GRPO and PPO fall below the base model on average. RLVB also outperforms PPO with a process reward from an LLM judge by 9.3% on average while using 1.6 times fewer GPU-hours. The trained agent answers with sufficient evidence in 51%, 12% and 74% of the episodes on the three benchmarks, against 8%, 0.4% and 36% for the base model. Its accuracy gain therefore comes from answering more often at high levels of evidence. The belief line shows how the agent reaches this evidence: it acts on its own belief. It commits at only 0.1 to 2.2% of the turns at which the evidence is insufficient and its line still names several answers, against 3 to 68% for the base model.

</details>

- What: AIエージェントが十分な証拠なしに早期に回答を確定してしまう「怠惰さ」の問題に対処するため、検証可能な信念に基づく強化学習(RLVB)を提案した。エージェントが信念が十分になるまで追加ステップを踏むことを報酬する。
- Novelty: エージェントの信念が十分であることをシミュレーターが計算し、それに基づいて報酬を与える点が新しい。外部の判断者や報酬モデルを必要としない。
- Why it matters: 長期タスクにおいて、AIエージェントが証拠に基づいて慎重に判断を下すようになり、より信頼性の高い意思決定を行うようになる。

## 7. iWAM: Efficient Action Prediction from World Models without Explicit Future Prediction
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.33141v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.33141v1
- Authors: Vansh Kapoor, Max Sobol Mark, Sreyas Venkataraman, Max Simchowitz, Aviral Kumar
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

World-action models (WAMs) build policies on pretrained video-generation models, on the premise that predicting how a scene evolves helps determine how to act. However, generating future scenes is expensive for real-time control. This motivates efficient proxies for future imagination, but how to train such proxies without sacrificing action-prediction quality remains unclear. An effective proxy need not generate a high-resolution frame; it only needs to provide enough information to distinguish between possible futures. This leads to the design principle behind iWAM: rather than denoising an entire future frame, can we condition the action policy on intermediate features from a single video-model evaluation? We show that the answer is yes. Exploiting the reversible dynamics of flow-matching models, we invert each training future into the corresponding latent noise vector. However, a single velocity evaluation at this latent does not necessarily yield informative features for action prediction. We therefore learn a bidirectional flow map that amortizes future-generation computation, enabling a single model evaluation to provide effective conditioning features. At inference, we sample a latent noise vector from the prior and condition the policy using just one video-backbone forward pass. Across simulated benchmarks and real robots, iWAM recovers the generalization benefits of WAMs while substantially reducing its inference cost.

</details>

- What: 世界モデルを用いた効率的な行動予測手法iWAMを提案した。
- Novelty: 明示的な未来予測を行わず、単一のビデオモデル評価から得られる中間特徴量を用いて行動ポリシーを条件付ける。

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Can a simple mirror expose the limitations of today's robotic manipulation policies?
