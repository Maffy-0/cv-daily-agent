## 1. It Takes Two to Tangram: Multi-Agent Object Centric Reinforcement Learning for Assembly
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.08814v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.08814v1
- Authors: Paul Knaußt, Kay Pompetzki, Nico Bohlinger, Niklas Funk, Cristiana de Farias, Rolf Findeisen, Jan Peters
- Keyword score: 6 / hits: reinforcement learning, multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Geometric assembly requires fine spatiotemporal coordination to place parts without causing collisions or deadlocks under tight clearances. To address this, we present an object-centric reinforcement learning framework that treats each part as an agent and learns a shared policy for continuous assembly control. A graph neural network represents the pieces, their target poses, and their spatial relationships, and predicts translational and angular velocities for each component. Per-agent proximal policy optimization with a centralized graph critic directly learns motion and temporal coordination within a single feedback policy. We study this formulation through planar Tangram assembly, where pieces must slide and rotate into a given configuration while remaining on the assembly surface. Procedural generation of target configurations and randomized initial arrangements support training and evaluation across diverse assembly problems. Our experiments isolate the roles of relational structure and multi-agent optimization, and compare learned control with sampling-based planning. We demonstrate concurrent assembly in simulation and transfer the policy to sequential execution on a real robot, as our object-centric formulation decouples learned assembly coordination from low-level physical control. ## 2. SURE:Spatiotemporal Uncertainty Representation for Efficient Vision-Language-Action Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09684v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09684v1
- Authors: Jingye Zhang, Choo Sin Wai, Dake Zhong, Zhifang Liu, Yuhong Zhang, Siyao Xiao, Guorui Song, Guocun Wang...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies have become a promising paradigm for end-to-end robot control, but their effectiveness still depends heavily on large volumes of demonstration data. In manipulation tasks, this dependence is compounded by structured spatiotemporal uncertainty in the action space: multiple nearby actions or trajectories may successfully solve the same task, while semantically equivalent demonstrations often unfold at different speeds. Standard pointwise supervision treats each demonstrated action as an exact target, imposing an unnecessary learning burden under such uncertainty. We present SURE, a data-efficient VLA training framework that explicitly models both spatial and temporal uncertainty. SURE augments a VLA backbone with a lightweight transposed-convolutional probabilistic action decoder that captures spatial uncertainty through local correlations among neighboring action bins. It further introduces Soft-DTW alignment supervision to accommodate local timing variations between predicted and demonstrated action chunks. Together, these two components reduce sensitivity to demonstrator-specific poses and execution timing, enabling more data-efficient action learning. We evaluate SURE on LIBERO, RoboTwin 2.0, and real-world manipulation tasks. Even with only 10% of the demonstration data or 20% of the training steps, SURE achieves manipulation success rates that match or surpass baseline VLAs.

</details>

## 3. Autonomous Particle Image Velocimetry Using Zero-Shot Sim-to-Real Reinforcement Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09679v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09679v1
- Authors: Chang Rajani, Luke Sebastien Marsden, Stephan Kunz, Claudio Mucignat, Ivan Lunati
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Particle image velocimetry (PIV) reconstructs a flow field from the motion of tracer particles but the reconstruction quality is bounded by how well the flow structure of interest is resolved in the images. The acquisition position is typically fixed by the operator before the measurement. We cast camera placement as a sequential decision-making problem and train a reinforcement learning agent to control a 6-DoF manipulator equipped with a camera, enabling it to locate, frame and resolve a hydrofoil wake in a water tunnel. The agent commands the camera pose and observes the scene only through the velocity field and uncertainty estimated from time-separated image pairs. It is trained entirely in a photorealistic simulation of our water tunnel, with wakes generated by Computational Fluid Dynamics (CFD) simulations of hydrofoils with varying profiles, positions and angles of attack. After zero-shot transfer to the real water tunnel, the policy reliably locates and frames the wakes of randomly placed hydrofoils. It resolves the wake at a higher resolution than a fixed camera view and with lower error in the measured flow statistics, in roughly half the time of a full scan and with far fewer image pairs.

## 4. Alignment by Confident Debate: Preference Learning from Multi-Agent Debate
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09568v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09568v1
- Authors: Nikhil Verma, Dingxian Wang, Mohammad Reza Rezaei, Andrew Rabinovich, Zhao Chen
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Post-training language models for reasoning typically rely on gold answers, task-specific verifiers, or learned reward models, limiting scalability when such signals are scarce. Multi-agent debate offers a self-supervised alternative, but converting debate into reliable training supervision remains challenging: majority voting weights agreeing responses equally, lacks a principled tie-breaking rule, and is ill-defined for open-ended tasks without a canonical answer; likelihood-based scoring can conflate fluency with correctness; and homogeneous committees may reinforce correlated errors. We introduce Alignment by Confident Debate (AbCD), which converts debate traces into preference data without gold answer labels. A sampling-diverse committee debates each problem, after which final answers are clustered by task-level semantic equivalence. We use verifiable tasks (mathematical reasoning, multiple-choice quantitative QA, and executable code-generation) as a controlled test bed: their answer keys or executable checkers let us evaluate the quality of debate-derived supervision against ground truth, even though the oracle itself never uses gold labels. Post-training on the resulting preferences outperforms inference-only ensembling and debate, as well as SFT on the same traces, across five model families spanning 1B to 8B parameters and four datasets, improving consensus accuracy, with a maximum gain of $21.5_{pp}$. Ablations show that the approach transfers to a larger model and to code generation, while also revealing limitations of heterogeneous committees and agreement-based weighting. Overall, the results suggest that debate traces can yield useful preference supervision when answer equivalence is combined with confidence-weighted disagreement.

</details>

## 5. DexDICE: Reconciling Safety and Performance via Dual-Expert Offline Imitation Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09433v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09433v1
- Authors: Seokin Seo, Sung Kuk Shyn, Minji Seo, Yisoo Lee, Jongmin Lee, Hongseok Yang, Kee-Eung Kim
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: imitation learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Ensuring high returns while providing strong safety guarantees in offline imitation learning (IL) is a fundamental challenge: existing approaches either rely on explicitly specified cost functions, which are difficult to design for complex real-world safety constraints, or require expert demonstrations that are both safe and high-performing, which are often too costly to collect. We introduce dual-expert imitation learning, which leverages two complementary data sources: (i) safety-compliant demonstrations that faithfully follow safety guidelines but may be suboptimal, and (ii) performance-oriented demonstrations that achieve high returns but may violate safety guidelines. To address this problem, we propose DexDICE (Dual-expert offline imitation learning via stationary DIstribution Correction Estimation). Instead of conventional distribution constraints, DexDICE combines DICE-style offline learning with safe support constraints, enabling agents to exploit high-return behaviors while remaining within safety-compliant regions. We prove that the ideal solution satisfying this constraint exactly has zero unsafe-region visitation with high probability. We evaluate DexDICE on a re-curated DSRL benchmark and validate it on a real-world mobile-robot navigation task trained directly from human teleoperation demonstrations, where it substantially outperforms baselines and remains robust to data scarcity and quality degradation.

</details>

## 7. Safe Circumnavigation on Heterogeneous Orbits: A Range-Only HOCBF-QP Framework for Nonholonomic Multi-Agent Systems
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.09112v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09112v1
- Authors: Shubham Singh, Anand Singh, Anjali Tiwari, Lara Briñón-Arranz, Katsuki Fujisawa
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Circumnavigating a stationary target with a team of robots requires each robot to hold its own standoff distance, to stay evenly spaced from its neighbours, and to avoid collisions with the target and with each other at every instant. The task becomes arduous when the robots are assigned different orbital radii, as their sensors or roles may demand. This paper presents a safety-critical control framework for this problem. Each robot commands both its acceleration and its turn-rate, measures only its distance to the target, and exchanges information only with its two ring neighbours. The design has three features. First, a structural property of the model lets one control law serve every robot regardless of its assigned radius. Second, the acceleration drives each radius exactly to its target, while the turn-rate balances the formation with the neighbours. Third, a higher-order control-barrier-function quadratic program enforces target, geofence, and inter-robot safety on both inputs at once; it leaves the nominal controller untouched away from the constraints and stays valid while the unmeasured range rate is still being estimated. Simulations confirm these guarantees. In a comparison, the closest existing multi-orbit design, even with full state information, violates both the target standoff and the inter-robot margin, whereas the proposed controller does not.

</details>

## 10. Grounding-Aware Judge Reward Reinforcement Learning for Out-of-Domain Face Anti-Spoofing

- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.09047v1
- Authors: Xiaoyong Yu, Xinge Yu

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Face Anti-Spoofing (FAS) must distinguish genuine faces from presentation attacks. A unified Multimodal Large Language Model (MLLM) is an attractive solution: it detects physical attacks (print, replay, mask) in one model and produces interpretable forensic evidence via Chain-of-Thought (CoT). A central obstacle to post-training such a model with reinforcement learning is that FAS reasoning has no rule-verifiable ground truth: only the binary verdict is checkable, while the reasoning chain cannot be verified by any hand-crafted rule. Optimizing the verdict alone is therefore a sparse, hackable signal. The model can reach the correct answer through a shortcut such as a false-rejection bias, which does not generalize. It is the judge, rather than the RL objective, that supplies the process supervision a verdict-only reward cannot. Our study starts from a model that already performs well In-Domain (ID) after CoT-based supervised fine-tuning and asks how to improve its Out-of-Domain (OOD) generalization. We find that grounding-aware rewards are an effective means to that end. We validate the method on OOD generalization, where the reward reduces ACER on two physical benchmarks (LCC-FASD 0.288 to 0.273, MSU-MFSD 0.431 to 0.409) while preserving ID accuracy (CelebA-Spoof 0.033 to 0.035). Against ResNet-50/101 and CLIP-ViT-B/16 baselines, the MLLM dominates ID and matches or surpasses the strongest baseline OOD. To our knowledge this is the first use of LLM-as-judge process supervision for FAS.
