## 1. HoloSplat: Real-Time Token Streaming for Feed-Forward Human Reconstruction
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31140v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31140v1
- Authors: Chaofan Li, Qihan Ding, Shihan Wang, Zhehao Shen, Boyu Shen, Quanyu Chen, Haoyu Wu, Lan Xu...
- Keyword score: 6 / hits: streaming, real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Live free-viewpoint video streaming of humans enables immersive telepresence and interactive XR applications. However, this requires reconstructing each frame in real time, transmitting the data within strict bandwidth budgets, and decoding it on lightweight client GPUs. Existing approaches, whether relying on per-frame optimization or feed-forward inference, generate massive 3D Gaussian payloads that exceed practical streaming limits, while aggressive compression severely degrades rendering quality. In this work, we present HoloSplat, a real-time streaming framework for human-centric volumetric video. HoloSplat splits a feed-forward reconstructor before its Gaussian head, introducing a multi-rate streaming codec driven by server-side token compression and client-side token restoration. On the server side, we apply opacity-guided token pruning, PCA-based dimensionality reduction, and lossless geometry coding for token anchors. On the client side, a feature reconstruction hypernetwork is fused into the Gaussian head to recover compression-induced quality loss. Furthermore, we introduce Neural Session Calibration (NSC), which rapidly fine-tunes the Gaussian head online using incoming camera views during a warm-up phase. This resolves quality degradation from novel camera parameters and varying capture conditions with zero per-frame overhead. In our evaluations, HoloSplat averages 32.2 FPS on four RTX 5090 GPUs at 50 Mbit/s. It decodes each frame into 3D Gaussians in 22.1 ms on an RTX 3050 Ti laptop GPU, transmitting 22.8$\times$ less data than uncompressed float32 Gaussians, while calibrated decoding improves PSNR by 1.050 dB.

</details>

## 2. LiveAudio: From Bidirectional to Autoregressive Diffusion for Real-Time Streaming Video-to-Audio Generation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30714v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30714v1
- Authors: Yuxuan Jiang, Zehua Chen, Yusheng Dai, Andong Wang, Weibei Dou, Jun Zhu
- Keyword score: 6 / hits: streaming, real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Video-to-audio generation synthesizes sound that matches a video in content and timing, with applications ranging from film post-production to live streaming, games and interactive world models. Interactive applications receive video continuously and need the sound to follow it in real time, yet most existing video-to-audio models are bidirectional and must observe an entire clip before producing any sound. We propose LiveAudio, a streaming video-to-audio framework that generates high-quality and synchronized audio in real time while the video is playing. In contrast to prior streaming models trained from scratch, LiveAudio converts a bidirectional model into a streaming generator so as to build on its audio quality and synchronization. LiveAudio generates audio block by block, and each block depends only on the video that has already been played. The streaming generator is obtained from the bidirectional model through progressive causal fine-tuning and distillation, so that each block needs only a few sampling steps. Experiments on VGGSound show that LiveAudio outperforms existing streaming models in audio quality and synchronization, and surpasses the bidirectional model it starts from in synchronization. On a single A100 GPU, it runs in real time with a real-time factor of 0.79 and a time to first frame of 143 ms. Audio samples are available at https://streamaudio.github.io.

</details>

## 3. SVUBench: A Comprehensive Benchmark for Proactive Streaming Video Understanding
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30541v1

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Proactive streaming video understanding requires models to interpret unfolding events, respond timely, and retrieve relevant historical evidence. To systematically evaluate these capabilities, we introduce SVUBench, a comprehensive proactive streaming video benchmark comprising 12,131 questions and 62,881 answer points across ten proactive tasks spanning Perception, Understanding, and Reasoning. Constructed through model-assisted annotation and human verification, SVUBench introduces two complementary interaction modes. Basic evaluates sustained proactive responses from video onset, while Trajectory requires historical recovery and continuous response updates following task activation during streaming. A Duration-Scaled Challenge further evaluates these capabilities across four video duration tiers. We develop the Proactive Response Score (PRS) with task-adaptive answer windows to jointly assess answer correctness, response timeliness, historical consistency, and content quality. Extensive experiments and analysis reveal the strengths and limitations of existing models, highlighting directions for advancing proactive streaming video understanding.

## 4. GlanceNav: Efficient Vision-and-Language Navigation with Streaming Memory and Counterfactual Rewind
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30900v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30900v1
- Authors: Anh Dao, Quan-Dung Pham, Danh Vinh Le, Tuyen Pham Le, Quan Nguyen, Yuzhang Shang
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: streaming, reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multimodal large language models (MLLMs) have become strong policies for vision-and-language navigation (VLN). However, most of them encode a sample of past frames again at every decision and are trained on isolated decisions, so computation is redundant and the usable history is short. They also learn from their own mistakes through outcome rewards that give one scalar to a whole episode. We ask whether a navigator can encode each frame only once, at a constant cost per step, and still remember what matters and learn efficiently from its mistakes. We present GlanceNav, a streaming navigator built on a hybrid MLLM. Its Gated DeltaNet layers carry a recurrent state through the whole episode, while its attention layers keep a short window of recent observations and a small bank of keyframes, selected by how much each frame changes the recurrent state. This constant-size memory keeps the cost per step fixed and allows training on whole episodes with the same memory as at inference. Because the entire memory can also be copied in constant time, we further propose Counterfactual Rewind (CoRe), a reinforcement learning stage that rewinds a drifting rollout to its closest approach to the goal, lets an expert correct a few actions, and credits decisions by the resulting change in outcome. Trained only on the R2R-CE and RxR-CE training splits, with 1,000 instructions for reinforcement learning, a 4B GlanceNav reaches success rates of 71.9% and 73.6% on R2R-CE and RxR-CE val-unseen. On R2R-CE, it outperforms the strongest previous single-RGB method trained without external data by 9.1 points, while running at 104 ms per step.

</details>

**LLM Summary**

- What: ストリーミングメモリとカウンターファクチュアルリワインドを備えた効率的な視覚言語ナビゲーション（VLN）エージェント「GlanceNav」を提案。
- Novelty: ゲート付きデルタネット層でエピソード全体を伝播するリカレント状態と、最近の観測の短いウィンドウ、およびキーフレームの小さなバンクを保持するアテンション層を組み合わせたハイブリッドMLLMを採用。カウンターファクチュアルリワインド（CoRe）による強化学習手法を提案。
- Why it matters: 各フレームのエンコードを一度に抑え、一定のコストでナビゲーションを行い、効率的な学習を可能にする。

## 5. DelayOPSD: On-Policy Self-Distillation from a Synchronous Teacher for Real-time Asynchronous Policies
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30534v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30534v1
- Authors: Hyeonjae Kim, Jeongsol Kim, Sunwoo Park, Chanyu Moon, Yoojin Oh, Youngjun Jun, Kyumin Choi, Jong Chul Ye
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: real-time, vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Flow-based vision-language-action (VLA) policies incur substantial inference latency due to their large vision-language backbones and iterative denoising. Asynchronous execution reduces this latency by generating the next action chunk while executing the current one, but the new chunk is conditioned on a stale observation, degrading performance as the delay increases. We investigate this mismatch by comparing actions predicted from observations at planning and execution times. We find that these predictions differ little on expert demonstrations but diverge substantially on the policy's own delayed rollouts, particularly in failed episodes. Motivated by this observation, we propose DelayOPSD, which trains an asynchronous policy to imitate its synchronous counterpart on the policy's own rollouts. Specifically, a synchronous teacher, obtained from the same policy using the observation at execution time, supervises a delayed student that observes only the earlier frame and the actions still being executed. The student learns to match the teacher throughout its own denoising process. Under a 10-step delay, training SmolVLA on demonstrations achieves only 70.2% success, comparable to naive asynchronous execution (70.4%), whereas training on its own rollouts improves success to 78.7%. Across LIBERO and Kinetix, DelayOPSD achieves the highest average success among the compared methods, without introducing additional modules at deployment. On a real robot, it further improves success from 46% to 62%.

</details>

- What: リアルタイム非同期ポリシーのためのオンポリシー自己蒸留手法「DelayOPSD」を提案。
- Novelty: 非同期ポリシーが、自身のロールアウトにおいて、実行時間での観測から得られる同期教師の予測を模倣するように学習させる。

## 6. OVOGO: Offline Value-Guided On-Policy Generative Optimization for Flow-Based Vision-Language-Action Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.30477v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.30477v1
- Authors: Rongzheng Yang, Jiaming Guo, Yuxuan Guo, Zihao Zhang, Yunkai Gao, Siming Lan, Shaohui Peng, Rui Zhang...
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: vision-language-action, reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Flow-based vision-language-action models can benefit from robot experience collected during deployment, but repeatedly acquiring new physical interactions for policy improvement is costly. Offline reinforcement learning provides a natural way to reuse collected experience, while flow-based policies offer an additional opportunity because fresh generation trajectories can be cheaply resampled from the current policy at replay observations. This motivates separating offline value learning from on-policy optimization of the generative process. We propose OVOGO, an offline reinforcement learning framework that realizes this separation for flow-based VLAs. A central challenge in this formulation is to learn a critic that remains grounded in logged robot experience while providing useful value signals for newly generated actions. OVOGO introduces a Return-Anchored Ranking Critic that combines in-sample distributional value learning with guarded local ranking, improving discrimination among generated actions without assigning them artificial return targets. The frozen critic then provides group-relative value signals for selected-transition optimization of fresh action candidates sampled from the current flow policy. Experiments on RoboTwin 2.0, DexJoCo, and LIBERO, as well as a real-robot dexterous manipulation task, show that OVOGO improves the initial VLA across all four settings and achieves higher success rates than the evaluated offline adaptation and value-guided baselines. These results suggest that offline robot experience can be repeatedly reused for value learning while inexpensive generative sampling supports policy optimization, providing an effective approach to experience-based post-training of flow-based VLAs.

</details>

## 7. VITA-WAM: Manifold-Aligned World-Action Modeling for Real-Time Robot Control
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31195v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31195v1
- Authors: Boqi Zhao, Dechen Gao, Hanchu Zhou, Hang Wang, Junshan Zhang
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

World action model (WAM) is a promising paradigm for visuomotor control by jointly modeling world dynamics and actions. Notably, existing WAMs often rely on large generative models and iterative sampling which incur substantial latency for action generation, limiting real-time deployment. In particular, there are two root causes of inefficiency in conventional flow matching or diffusion based action models in WAMs: 1) transporting uninformative Gaussian noise into precise actions necessitating multiple ODE steps, and 2) repeatedly injecting task context through expensive cross-attention modules. Our key insight is that jointly learning and aligning the source and action manifolds streamlines the transport between them, substantially accelerating action generation. Building on this insight, we introduce VITA-WAM, a WAM architecture with manifold-aligned flow-matching (VITA flow matching) that directly transports latent observation representations containing visual, language, and proprioceptive information into latent actions. More specifically, a flow latent alignment (FLA) mechanism is employed to facilitate manifold alignment between latent observation and action representations, enabling accurate generation with few ODE steps. The resulting architecture also eliminates costly attention for observation conditioning during repeated denoising steps. Notably, with a single ODE step, VITA-WAM outperforms 10-step FastWAM by 21.47 pp on LIBERO-Plus and by 1.65 pp on LIBERO, achieving these gains through the FLA objective without distillation, while inferring at 55.67~ms latency, a $2.10\times$ speedup compared to FastWAM.

## 9. Self-Upgrading Embodied Agents
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.31117v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.31117v1
- Authors: Jiajun Fan, Neil He, Qinglong Zheng, Changyi Yang, Tong Wei, Andy Mao, Zetian Li, Chaoran Cheng...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: embodied agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

An embodied agent built on a language model goes to work with the weights it was given and keeps them for life. To get better, it needs more training, or reflection, memory, search or prompts that cost every step and are lost when the task changes. Editing weights by reasoning, weight-space programming, could end this, but has stayed with models that only reason or relied on a far larger commercial model. We introduce the self-upgrading embodied agent, to our knowledge the first that learns from its own experience by upgrading its own weights on the job, between its tasks, with no training, no gradient, no reward model, no second model, no test-time scaling and no harness or prompt engineering. By day a 27B agent works in a household environment. At night it reads its record, finds many of its steps wasted on replies that reach the token limit before any action, traces the habit to one direction in its hidden states, and rewrites one tensor of its weights. An agent that rewrites itself must first never break itself, so it is its own examiner: it tests every change against its unchanged self on practice tasks and keeps only what holds. When an edit holds, the next day it is a better agent, at the same cost per step: over thousands of held-out episodes, 48 fresh seeds a task, its one kept edit makes it complete significantly more tasks, in fewer steps, with fewer wasted and fewer tokens. This self-improvement is multi-round, stable and continual: through the nights that follow, the upgrade holds, without collapse. Of four sizes of its family, only the 27B agent does this. From here on, an embodied agent need not keep training and inference apart: as it works, it gathers its own experience and upgrades itself, with no separate pre-training or post-training and no human intervention. We believe this opens lifelong continual learning for robots: stable, continual, recursive self-improvement.

</details>

- What: 自己アップグレード可能な具現化エージェントを提案。トレーニング、勾配、報酬モデル、第二モデル、テスト時スケーリング、プロンプトエンジニアリングなしに、自身の経験から学習し、自身の重みをアップグレードする。

Scaling visuo-tactile policy learning is constrained by the high cost of real-world data collection and the difficulty of building realistic simulations. Generative models offer a scalable source of training data, yet useful demonstrations require plausible tactile feedback, temporal alignment with visual contact, and accurate actions.
