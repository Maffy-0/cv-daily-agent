## 1. Efficient VLA Reinforcement Learning via Subchunk Rollouts in Latent World Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27645v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27645v1
- Authors: Nan Tang, Han-Tao Zhang, Jing-Cheng Pang, Hao-Nan Wang, Hao-Xiang Ren, Yi-Fei Sheng, Jun-Yin Ye, Tian Xu...
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: vision-language-action, reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Model-based reinforcement learning (MBRL) can improve pretrained vision-language-action (VLA) policies through imagined experiences. However, existing VLA-MBRL methods primarily rely on large-scale image or video generation world models, incurring large computational overhead. Latent world models offer a more efficient alternative but face two challenges: compounding errors during recursive prediction and incompatibility with VLAs' visual input interfaces. In this work, we propose LS-MBPO, a lightweight residual MBRL framework that leverages latent world models to improve upon pretrained VLAs. LS-MBPO introduces two key techniques: (1) Hierarchical Forcing, which combines dense causal supervision with autoregressive feedback to mitigate compounding prediction errors; and (2) Latent Subchunk Rollouts, which leverage action chunks from a frozen VLA policy and a latent residual policy for successive subchunk refinement, bridging the gap between latent imagination and VLA policy execution. We further introduce a Progress-Conditioned Branch-Start Curriculum that progressively shifts rollout starting points from later to earlier task stages, enabling effective policy learning from short imagined rollouts. Extensive experiments on LIBERO, RoboTwin, and a real-world ALOHA robot show that LS-MBPO achieves superior task performance and training efficiency compared to existing methods.

</details>

## 2. Opto-Electronic Hybrid Reinforcement Learning Based on MZI for Robotic Manipulator Control
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27129v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27129v1
- Authors: Mengting Yu, Shuiying Xiang, Changjian Xie, Yonghang Chen, Haowen Zhao, Xingxing Guo, Zhijian Hao, Yahui Zhang...
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: real-time, reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Precise motion control for autonomous robots is a core technical enabler for the large-scale deployment of embodied intelligence. In particular, basic manipulation tasks, such as point-to-point reaching and target grasping with robotic manipulators, impose stringent requirements on control accuracy, real-time response, and energy efficiency for edge deployment. As a prevailing end-to-end control paradigm, reinforcement learning (RL) has demonstrated remarkable capabilities in autonomous learning and generalization for decision-making in continuous action spaces. Nevertheless, constrained by electronic computing architectures, it generally suffers from substantial computing overhead, high inference latency and limited energy efficiency, making low-power edge deployment challenging. Optical computing, benefiting from its high-speed parallelism, offers a promising route to break energy-efficiency bottlenecks; however, research on the integration of optical computing and RL remains at an early stage. In this work, we propose a Mach-Zehnder Interferometer (MZI)-based opto-electronic hybrid RL architecture. We adopted two algorithm structures: Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient combined with Hindsight Experience Replay (DDPG-HER) and Twin Delayed Deep Deterministic Policy Gradient combined with HER (TD3-HER). We implemented the key layer linear computation of the spiking neural network (SNN) Actor on an optical MZI chip for the reaching task of a Franka Emika Panda robotic manipulator. The architecture is evaluated in the PyBullet simulation environment. Experimental results demonstrate that the proposed architecture achieves an energy efficiency of 1.39 TOPS/W and an inference latency of 120ps while maintaining control accuracy, which provide a feasible pathway toward energy-efficient and precise control for robots.

## 6. Budgeted Safety Verification for Streaming Video Generation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27268v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27268v1
- Authors: Yuxin Cao, Ziqi Ding, Jiayu Zhang, Xianglin Yang, Wei Song, Jin Song Dong
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: streaming

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

A streamed video generator releases each chunk the moment that it is produced, so a safety decision that waits for the finished clip arrives after the harm has been seen. We measure a guardrail by exposure, the harmful frames a viewer is shown, and ask where a fixed moderation budget should be spent. The accurate detector reads decoded frames with a video language model and is too expensive for every chunk, so our guard reads hidden states the generator has computed already and spends a cheap score on deciding when to call it. On 599 streams from a distilled causal generator the undefended model shows 46.25 harmful frames of 81. The video model on every chunk leaves 0.91 frames at 80.1 scored per benign stream, and the same model triggered by our guard leaves 2.35 at 5.3. Spending that budget at random leaves 37.20 frames, so the gain is in the ordering, and a frame detector in the same role gives up almost six, so the judging stage must read the chunk as a clip. The ordering is worth more as the stream lengthens, and the guard carries to a second system. An independent guardrail model agrees with our oracle at $κ=0.74$, and a blinded audit by 50 participants puts its precision at 96.1% and recall at 82.8%. Warning. This paper contains examples of generated content that some readers may find disturbing or offensive in nature.

</details>

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Visual labeling of egocentric demonstrations often leaves contiguous gaps in action supervision. Reliable poses around a gap still provide its net translation and rotation. ## 9. Tripwire: Robot Agents that Program Their Own Interrupts under Human Disturbance
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.27182v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.27182v1
- Authors: Sunli Chen, Yun Zong, Qinhong Zhou, Xiaowen Qiu, Jiaben Chen, Zeyuan Yang, Chuang Gan
- Keyword score: 2 / hits: vision-language-action, robot policy

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Agent harnesses are turn-based: the agent acts, waits for the result, then reasons. When the action invokes a pretrained robot policy, such as a vision-language-action (VLA) or world action model (WAM), the policy may react continuously to observations, but the agent cannot revisit its decision until the call returns. Meanwhile, a person may step into the robot's path or move the object it is about to grasp. Invoking the agent more often narrows this blind interval, but a reasoning model takes seconds per decision and most control steps need no new one. We propose TRIP, a training-free, event-driven harness in which the agent programs its own interrupts. With each call, the agent writes a tripwire: a lightweight, stateful Python program that runs alongside the policy, can query a compact VLM (for example, to locate a person and measure their distance), and decides whether the call continues or control returns to the agent. When it fires, the harness halts the policy and returns control with the reason; the agent then reassesses, adapts, and continues under a rewritten tripwire. We evaluate TRIP with three VLAs and one WAM on RoboHD, a benchmark of 168 RoboCasa365 scenes in which a simulated person disturbs the robot and any human-robot contact counts as failure. Compared with a turn-based harness using the same agent and tools, TRIP halves the rate of human contact with the strongest policy, raising safety from 61.3% to 81.3%, without sacrificing task success (38.7% vs. 31.0%). Real-robot experiments show that TRIP completes two manipulation tasks under human interference where the turn-based harness and the policy alone largely fail.

</details>
