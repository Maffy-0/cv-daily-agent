## 1. MARGoS: A Configuration-Driven Platform for Reproducible MARL Experiments in ARGoS
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13772v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13772v1
- Authors: Tom Philipp Backert, Mohammad D. Soorati, Javad Ghofrani
- Keyword score: 5 / hits: reinforcement learning, multi-agent, marl

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL) has become a prominent research direction, particularly within collective robotics, where the ARGoS simulator is widely used to study emergent swarm behavior. Yet despite this growth, systematic support for reuse and reproducibility in MARL-based swarm robotics remains limited and insufficiently standardized: a single experiment is assembled from manually aligned simulator files, training scripts, configuration values, paths, seeds, and environment details, so reuse and reproduction stay fragile even within one research group. To address this gap, we introduce MARGoS, a lightweight, configuration-driven platform developed in this project that elevates these experiment-defining inputs to first-class, platform-managed artifacts. MARGoS enables reproducible experimentation through configuration hashing, end-to-end seed propagation, frozen (immutable) configurations, structured logging, environment fingerprinting, and exportable experiment bundles that facilitate sharing, replay, and verification of results across machines and over time. A five-dimension evaluation (architecture, efficiency, reproducibility, usability, and collaboration) supports our claim that managing experiment variation as a platform responsibility makes ARGoS+MARL experiments easier to reuse, reproduce, and hand off. Our code is available at https://github.com/tombackert/margos.

</details>

## 2. MAGMA-Bench: Evaluating Long-Horizon Embodied Agents under Dynamic Tasks and Execution Failures
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13534v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13534v1
- Authors: Loan Bernat, Matthieu Grard, Ariane Herbulot, Florent Lamiraux
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: embodied agent, multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-horizon embodied agents must maintain task-relevant information, coordinate heterogeneous robots, and adapt their decisions as execution unfolds. Existing benchmarks investigate dynamic task execution and embodied multi-agent coordination, but provide limited evaluation of their intersection. We introduce MAGMA-Bench, an architecture-agnostic benchmark for persistent task reasoning in dynamic multi-robot environments. MAGMA-Bench evaluates agents across increasing task horizons and controlled online perturbations, including execution failures, task interruptions, and mission updates, with scenarios requiring coordination across robots with heterogeneous capabilities. It uses a structured skill-level interface to separate task-level reasoning from perception and low-level control. We evaluate locally deployable open-weight planners, multi-agent state-tracking strategies, and frontier models. Even the strongest evaluated models fail on a substantial fraction of clean episodes, and their success rates decrease further under online perturbations. Supervised adaptation greatly improves a small model, but performance remains substantially lower on longer and multi-robot missions. We release the benchmark, 261,646 task-level training examples, and an architecture-agnostic expert-trace generation pipeline.

## 5. Stride: Scale-Aware Multi-Agent Evolutionary Refinement for Infographic Generation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13502v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13502v1
- Authors: Yan Zhou, Yang Yang
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Infographic generation challenges text-to-image models because a single image must satisfy numerous visual, textual, and semantic constraints. Iterative agentic refinement with a multimodal verifier is a natural remedy, but existing methods face a scale mismatch: local edits cannot correct global composition errors, while full regeneration may destroy already-correct regions. We introduce Stride, a constraint-grounded multi-agent framework that treats refinement over any frozen generator as a history-aware evolution strategy. Stride separates constraint evaluation - whether each requirement is satisfied - from visual grounding - where and how it is violated. It then routes global revisions to a composer subagent and local corrections to a retoucher subagent. Each subagent conducts internal planning and generates multiple candidates, while Stride accepts a candidate only when it improves the verifier score, reducing regression across iterations. Because stochastic generators and imperfect verifiers make any single step unreliable, it is this combination of scale-aware routing, per-step redundancy, and monotonic acceptance that yields stable improvement across iterations. With fully open-weight generator-verifier systems, Stride outperforms proprietary systems including Nano-Banana-2.0 and Seedream-5.0-Pro on BizGenEval, matches GPT-Image-2.0 and surpasses Seedream-5.0-Pro on IGenBench, and achieves a new state-of-the-art score on WISE, while substantially outperforming existing agentic methods across all evaluated benchmarks.

</details>

## 7. Redirecting Teacher Supervision: Temporal-Response-Guided Latent Distillation for Video-Language Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13045v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13045v1
- Authors: Xinkai Lu, Junli Zha, Haolan Wei, Jiahui Wang, Chao Zhou, Xiang Song
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: video-language

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

On-policy distillation (OPD) transfers capabilities through teacher predictions on student-generated sequences, as studied by GKD and MiniLLM for language models and Video-OPD for video tasks. We ask how internal supervision can further support temporal learning. Direct hidden-state alignment does not explicitly use the student's temporal responses to choose a supervision direction. We propose Temporal-Response Guidance (TRG), which measures representation responses to local temporal perturbations, adds the teacher correction's projection onto their span back to the original correction, and restores its original norm. The projection is not normalized separately, and correction components outside the response space are retained. With an orthogonal tangent projector and a detached target, TRG changes the supervision direction while preserving the cosine-loss value and aligned-representation gradient norm at target construction. On TempCompass, Video-MME, MVBench, and Video-MMMU, TRG improves the 4B student's macro accuracy by 2.86 percentage points over the reported output-only OPD baseline.

## 8. Reflect-VLA: Internalizing Reflective Guidance via Dual-Path On-Policy Self-Distillation for Autonomous Driving
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13453v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13453v1
- Authors: Yuechen Luo, He Chong, Fang Li, Shaoqing Xu, Hexin Zhang, Ruiqi Zhang, Ziying Song, Lei Yang...
- Keyword score: 2 / hits: vision-language-action, reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Reinforcement Learning (RL) substantially improves planning performance in autonomous-driving Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models. However, existing RL methods rely on scalar trajectory rewards that measure overall quality but fail to identify specific planning errors or provide actionable correction signals. In long-tail scenarios, errors in scene understanding, behavior selection, and trajectory execution are compressed into a single score. We observe that Reflective Guidance turns trajectory evaluation into explicit diagnoses and corrective instructions, substantially improving planning accuracy. Yet such guidance requires an initial prediction and its evaluation results, making it unavailable for direct single-pass inference. We propose Reflect-VLA, which treats Reflective Guidance as training-time privileged information and internalizes its planning benefits through Dual-Path On-Policy Self-Distillation (Dual-OPSD). Unlike scalar-reward optimization, Dual-OPSD uses the driving score only to route student-generated trajectories. It distills dense token-level corrective distributions from the teacher for risky trajectories while applying self-imitation to preserve reliable ones. To provide reliable corrective distributions on student rollouts, the teacher is progressively constructed through Driving-Knowledge Pretraining, Planning-and-Refinement SFT, and Self-Refinement Rejection Sampling. At inference time, Reflect-VLA generates trajectories in a single pass without explicit guidance or iterative refinement. Experiments on NAVSIM v1/v2 and Bench2Drive demonstrate competitive planning performance while reducing training time by over 90% compared with RL.

</details>

## 9. Splat2Act: From Captured Scenes to Personalized VLA Manipulation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13945v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13945v1

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

A user who brings a generalist manipulation policy into their home or office wants it to pick up their objects from their shelves, counters, and tables. Yet even a strong generalist like $π_{0.5}$ achieves 0% pickup success in our captured deployment scenes without adaptation, and collecting real demonstrations in every user's environment is prohibitively expensive. Deployed on a real Stretch 3, the same policy achieves 63.9% success on a 12-task subset, closely tracking its 66.7% simulated success on those tasks. These results suggest that automated real-to-sim-to-real training can personalize VLA manipulation policies using only simulation-oracle data.

## 13. OWLET: On-Policy Weighted Learning via Evidence Transfer for Video Large Language Model Post-Training
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13272v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13272v1
- Authors: Qitan Lv, Ke Fan, Hong Wang, Wen Wu, Yilin Wang, Bowen Zhou, Feng Wu, Chao Zhang
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Post-training has become a key stage for eliciting reasoning capabilities in Video Large Language Models (Video-LLMs). Existing post-training methods mainly follow two paradigms: reinforcement learning (RL) and on-policy distillation (OPD). However, they do not fully exploit visual evidence for effective training. RL supervises the policy with a single trajectory-level reward, which is too coarse-grained for video reasoning: the same response-level advantage is applied to every generated token, disregarding that the tokens of a video response are grounded in the visual stream to very different degrees. OPD provides token-level supervision through output-distribution matching, but lacks direct supervision of the spatiotemporal representations that produce those outputs. We observe across extensive experiments that the same model reasons more accurately when its video view combines global temporal context with question-relevant details. Comparing the same student-generated response under this view and uniform sampling reveals changes in token likelihoods and internal representations, providing a training signal for evidence use. Building on this observation, we propose OWLET, an evidence-privileged RL method that derives training signals from a richer teacher view. This view couples uniform global context with question-conditioned local windows, providing visual evidence without requiring a larger model. To better exploit this privileged visual evidence, OWLET introduces (i) \textbf{evidence-aware credit redistribution}, which compares token likelihoods across the two views and redistributes positive response-level advantages according to their relative evidence support; and (ii) \textbf{spatiotemporal state alignment}, which aligns the two views' hidden states on verifier-accepted responses.

## 14. From Glance to Focus: Mitigating Recognition Laziness in MLLMs for Fine-Grained Recognition
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13154v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13154v1
- Authors: Yutong Yang, Zhuqi Liu, Mouxing Yang, Xi Peng
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: action recognition

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multimodal Large Language Models (MLLMs) generally fall short of expectations in Fine-Grained Visual Recognition (FGVR), although they exhibit promising FGVR foundations, i.e., visual perception and broad category knowledge. Most existing studies seek to remedy this deficiency by adopting domain-specific post-training, at the risk of compromising generalization to unseen domains. Different from these efforts, in this paper, we revisit the FGVR mechanism underlying MLLMs from both the empirical and theoretical perspectives and uncover an inference-time phenomenon termed FGVR Laziness. In brief, FGVR Laziness refers to the insufficient association between visual cues and category concepts under certain candidate category lists and recognition guidance, leaving the FGVR foundations of MLLMs largely under-utilized. To mitigate FGVR Laziness, we propose Laziness-resIstant Visual rEcognition (LIVE), a training-free framework which jointly refines candidate lists and recognition guidance to elicit the latent FGVR capability of MLLMs. Specifically, LIVE first performs query-adaptive candidate inference to derive a compact yet reliable candidate list. Meanwhile, LIVE constructs category-specific FGVR skills by mining the intra-category characteristics and inter-category differences. Finally, LIVE selectively invokes detailed skill guidance according to recognition uncertainty and category confusion, shifting MLLM-based FGVR from a cursory glance to a focus on associating discriminative visual cues with category concepts. Extensive experiments on 13 benchmarks against 24 state-of-the-art methods spanning fine-grained category recognition, action recognition, and general category recognition, verify the effectiveness and generalizability of LIVE across different MLLM backbones. The code will be released upon acceptance.

## 15. Forward Dynamics Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.13117v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.13117v1
- Authors: Zipei Ma, Junzhe Jiang, Jiahui Zhang, Juntong Zhang, Chun Gu, Bozhou Zhang, Jiankang Deng, Xiatian Zhu...
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-language-action (VLA) models and world action models (WAMs) have advanced robotic policy learning by building on pretrained vision-language models (VLMs) and video generative models (VGMs), respectively. Yet VLAs learn without explicit supervision from future world states, while WAMs that condition actions on predicted futures incur extra inference cost and let visual prediction errors propagate into actions. We present Forward Dynamics Models (FDM), a family of robotic action models that couples action and future world-state prediction through an action-to-future paradigm. Future prediction is conditioned on action features, never the reverse: end-to-end training lets ground-truth future states supervise the policy through these features, while prediction errors cannot propagate into actions. At inference, FDM skips future generation entirely, avoiding its computational cost. The paradigm is backbone-agnostic, and we instantiate it with both VLM and VGM backbones. Experiments in simulation and on real robots demonstrate the effectiveness of FDM across both backbone families, achieving a state-of-the-art 99.4% average success rate on LIBERO and absolute gains of up to 13% over VLA and WAM methods on real-world tasks.

</details>
