## 1. LiveAct: Towards Real-Time Multi-Agent World Models with Heterogeneous Action Interfaces
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29408v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29408v1
- Authors: Xinyu Wang, Huafeng Shi, Yue Ma, Zijie Meng, Teng Xi, Yuzhuo Chen, Yucheng Wang, Ruicheng Zhang...
- Keyword score: 7 / hits: streaming, real-time, multi-agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Interactive video world models predict how an environment evolves under user actions, but most existing systems assume one controlled agent and one task-specific action interface. We study multi-agent video world modeling, where one model generates a joint visual future for multiple controlled agents while interpreting heterogeneous controls ranging from binary game buttons to continuous robot commands. This task presents two complementary challenges. First, every agent-indexed action stream must control its intended visual entity independently. Second, the model must adapt the meaning of each control variable across different tasks, where the same tensor dimension may denote an attack, steering command, or robot command. We introduce LiveAct, centered on a Heterogeneous Action Interface (HAI) whose two components mirror this decomposition. To support independent control, Agent-Aware Spatial Modulation uses language-driven spatial gates and agent-specific attention to bind each action stream to its intended visual agent. To accommodate heterogeneous controls, Schema-Guided Action Adaptation describes each control variable in language and modulates its semantic token with the synchronized numerical value. We further transfer the complete interface to a blockwise causal generator through teacher forcing and rollout-aware distribution matching, enabling efficient streaming generation while retaining agent-specific control and action semantics. We instantiate the formulation on six multi-agent sources spanning games and multi-robot manipulation, and evaluate visual quality, action following, agent attribution, and latency. Across games and robot manipulation, LiveAct reaches 0.898 and 0.942 agent-attribution accuracy without agent locations or identity crops, and the causal generator attains 17.70 FPS at $512\times 448$.

</details>

## 2. ProactEgoMem: Benchmarking Proactive Memory in Streaming Egocentric Video
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30089v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30089v1
- Authors: Ruotong Liao, Meikel Kokowski, Xiaoxiao Sun, Yongqin Xian, Laura Bravo Sánchez, Shiye Su, Federico Tombari, Serena Yeung-Levy...
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: streaming, video-language

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Today's multimodal assistants are typically prompted to interpret the visual evidence presented to them. To offload the cognitive burden of remembering across long horizons, wearable assistants should instead proactively acquire and maintain useful memory as the user's surroundings evolve. Doing so on the user's behalf requires identifying what matters, understanding the scene in real time, and initiating memory maintenance in the background while the user stays focused on their activities. We introduce ProactEgoMem, a benchmark that evaluates proactive memory in streaming egocentric video. Three tasks ground the evaluation in ambient environmental needs: Information Capture (IC) preserves transient yet dense visual information, State Tracking (ST) follows changing object states, and Location Tracking (LT) records object movements. The benchmark defines its protocol at two levels of proactiveness: the Notification Level asks for a single timely notification that satisfies the user's intent, while the deeper Intervention Level asks the agent to maintain memory on the user's behalf through an active start-to-stop tool lifecycle. Parallel ProactEgoMem-Daily and ProactEgoMem-Sci tracks contain 300/100 clips covering daily and scientific use cases, paired with 1000/500 questions in ProactEgoMem-QA, which measures the quality of the proactively acquired memory. Across proprietary, open-source, video-language, and streaming baselines, even the strongest models fail to capture memory both timely and accurately. ProactEgoMem provides a rigorous evaluation foundation for proactive multimodal assistance and for video agents that co-exist with their users.

</details>

## 3. Distance Field Driven Reinforcement Learning for Reactive and Robust Collision Avoidance

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Robust and reactive collision avoidance remains a central challenge for deploying Reinforcement Learning (RL) policies on real robotic manipulators, particularly when transferring behaviours learned in simulation to the physical world. This paper presents an RL framework that integrates the Euclidean Distance Field (EDF) directly into the observation space, providing the agent with continuous geometric cues that support stable and responsive avoidance behaviours. By leveraging the rich spatial information encoded in the EDF, the method enables a streamlined reward formulation that achieves effective obstacle avoidance both with and without Artificial Potential Field guidance. The proposed approach is validated on a 7-DoF redundant manipulator, demonstrating zero-shot transfer from simulation to real hardware. Despite being trained exclusively with randomised static objects, the deployed policy generalizes to previously unseen real environments and exhibits robust online avoidance of dynamic obstacles. These results highlight the potential of EDF-based representations to mitigate the sim-to-real gap and support reliable and real-time collision-aware behaviour in complex and changing environments.

## 4. Plan First, Act Later: Streaming Reasoning for Executable Memory in Robot Control
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29329v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29329v1
- Authors: Qinghang Liu, Conglin Wang, Zongsheng Li, Boyong He, Han Gao, Pengcheng Wang, Tao Sun, Lige Liu...
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: streaming, vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Long-horizon robot manipulation requires a robot to remember task-relevant information beyond its short observation window. Existing memory-augmented policies typically retrieve relevant information from history and reason over it at execution time, placing a heavy reasoning burden on the high-level model. To address this challenge, we propose PAL (\textbf{P}lan first, \textbf{A}ct \textbf{L}ater), a hierarchical memory framework for vision-language robot manipulation. It maintains a short-term visual buffer, a semantic-event memory, and an executable \texttt{subtask\_list}. A high-level vision-language model (VLM) converts streaming visual observations into the \texttt{subtask\_list}. At execution time, the VLM selects the currently relevant subtask from the \texttt{subtask\_list} and passes it to a low-level vision-language-action (VLA) policy. To teach the model what to write to memory and when to update it, we introduce a two-stage data generation procedure that first expands sparse update supervision and then uses self-labeling with task-grounded verification to generate training data that better match the state distribution encountered during execution. We evaluate our approach on memory-dependent benchmarks and real-world manipulation tasks. On RoboMME, PAL achieves a \textbf{56.05\%} success rate, outperforming the strongest baseline, FrameSamp-Modul, at 44.51\%. In real-world experiments across four manipulation tasks, \pal{} reaches \textbf{60.83\%} overall success, surpassing MemER (45.00\%) and FrameSamp-Modul (28.33\%). These results show that proactively writing future-relevant subtasks into hierarchical memory improves long-horizon visual reasoning and robot control. Code and videos can be found on our website: https://qinghangliu.github.io/pal-webpage/

</details>

## 5. Multimodal Embodied Agents: A Unified Capability-centric Perspective from Computer-Use to Robot-Use
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30108v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30108v1
- Authors: Yanzhe Chen, Ziyi Yang, Jifeng Zhu, Qiming Huang, Ruihe An, Peiyao Xu, Hesen Yang, Runda Liu...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: embodied agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Multimodal agents (MMAs) sustain interaction through reasoning, memory, tools, and feedback, most visibly as computer-use agents, while robotic systems (RSs) couple sensing and actuation under physical dynamics. We define multimodal embodied agents (MMEAs) as goal-directed systems that couple multimodal task reasoning with physical action and revise decisions from the resulting feedback. This raises a central question: what changes when a multimodal agent moves from computer-use to robot-use? We introduce PAPAV, a unified capability-centric framework of five recurring functions: Perceive the current state, Anticipate action effects, Plan a feasible course, Act through an interface or body, and Verify the outcome. Defined by function rather than architecture, these capabilities let PAPAV identify what all three share and then compare MMEAs with MMAs and with RSs. Five physical constraints, from partial observability to unverifiable outcomes, leave fewer choices fixed in advance and less room to reverse errors. Across 64 benchmarks, explicit evaluation centers on Act; only 5 assess Anticipate and 3 assess Verify, leaving both largely hidden behind task success. These constraints also frame the open challenges we identify. PAPAV therefore provides a common basis for designing reliable physical agents and measuring progress across all five capabilities rather than task success alone.

</details>

## 7. Attn-Forcing: Learning Full-Context Attention Proxies for Streaming Video Generation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.30036v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.30036v1
- Authors: Junyao Gao, Sibo Liu, Jiaxing Li, Yanan Sun, Hanxin Zhu, Weidong Zhang, Jun Zhang, Cairong Zhao
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: streaming

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Increasing the attention context length significantly improves streaming video generation quality, while incurring prohibitive memory and computational costs as the context grows. Existing methods attempt to capture this contextual information through attention selection or compression, but remain limited by bounded representations and lack explicit attention-level supervision to retain historical contributions to current queries. To address this, we introduce Attn-Forcing, a novel framework that preserves full-context attention contributions for streaming video generation under bounded memory and computation. Moreover, we propose an Attention Matching Loss to explicitly constrain the attention behavior of the learned proxies using full-context attention supervision. Extensive qualitative and quantitative experiments demonstrate that Attn-Forcing consistently improves streaming video generation quality and achieves state-of-the-art performance over existing baseline methods.

## 8. GeoMem-VLA: Internalizing 3D Scene Understanding into Temporal Memory for Vision-Language-Action Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29866v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29866v1
- Authors: Qinfan Zhang, Yifan Luo, Haoyuan Shi, Guanlin Liu, Han Dong, Haozhe Shan, Yong Dai, Xiangcong Ren...
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Geometric structure is essential for robotic manipulation and understanding the 3D world. Most VLA models rely on 2D representations of current observations, while geometry-aware approaches often require additional 3D information. However, geometry inferred independently from individual observations is often discarded rather than accumulated into a persistent task representation. Our key insight is that action generation needs a compact, task-relevant geometric abstraction that can be continuously updated, rather than a complete, renderable 3D reconstruction at every step. We introduce GeoMem-VLA, a VLA framework that internalizes explicit 3D supervision into dedicated geometric tokens and maintains them as temporal memory for action generation, without directly aligning the VLM's native visual embeddings with the teacher feature space. After benchmark-specific downstream adaptation, GeoMem-VLA achieves 98.9% average success on LIBERO and 91.10% / 91.24% on RoboTwin's clean/randomized settings. ## 9. Post-hoc Epistemic Uncertainty for Vision-Language-Action Policies
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.29730v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.29730v1
- Authors: Johannes Keller, Albrecht M. Bloße, Adrian Lindenmeyer, Thomas Neumuth, Daniel Schneider
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-language-action (VLA) policies need to recognize gaps in their training knowledge to request useful demonstrations or assistance in new situations, especially when failures are costly. Disagreement within an ensemble of independently trained policies can guide these decisions, but requires training and running multiple policies. We propose post-hoc kernel-variance heads that estimate epistemic uncertainty from the embeddings of a single policy. Each head computes the conditional variance of a Gaussian process conditioned on embeddings from the training demonstrations. Fitting requires no regression targets or gradient updates and leaves the policy unchanged. In experiments with SmolVLA on LIBERO-10, the kernel variance heads perform competitively with two-policy ensemble scores in task-level correlation with task success, in demonstration acquisition during active learning, and in referral to a stronger supervisor policy as a proxy for human assistance. For SmolVLA, the head on the vision-language backbone reduces uncertainty-scoring time by up to approximately a factor of 50 compared with ensemble scores, while also substantially reducing setup costs. We also show that the established evaluation protocol on LIBERO-10 primarily rewards recognition of coverage differences between tasks, while execution variability weakens assessment of within-task rankings. Thus, controlled perturbations provide a complementary evaluation: modality-specific heads identify unfamiliar camera, instruction, or state inputs, while fused heads can detect unfamiliar combinations of individually familiar inputs. With a supervisor proxy trained on the evaluated perturbation families, the decoder head improves within-task referral over ensemble disagreement. These findings show that useful uncertainty estimates for adaptive robot learning can be obtained from a single policy.

</details>
