<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Coordinated whole-body control of humanoid robots remains challenging due to high-dimensional coupled dynamics and substantial physical heterogeneity across joint degrees of freedom (JDoFs). Existing multi-agent reinforcement learning methods either employ coarse agent partitions or directly apply generic heterogeneous-agent algorithms, while hierarchical controllers commonly optimize high-level planning and low-level execution independently, leading to uncoordinated motion. In this paper, we propose H^2MARL, a hierarchical heterogeneous multi-agent reinforcement learning framework with two key mechanisms. Extensive experiments in MuJoCo and Isaac Gym demonstrate that H^2MARL consistently improves locomotion stability, gait coordination, and motion quality over homogeneous, coarse-grained, and independently optimized alternatives.

## 2. Turns Matter: Advancing Streaming Video RL via Turn-Level Credit
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06728v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06728v1
- Authors: Shuai Dong, Kanghui Tian, Linghan Chen, Wenqi Shao, Hongrui Wu, Haowen Hou, Yongfu Zhu, Xuehui Wang...
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: streaming, video understanding

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Streaming video understanding requires models to repeatedly decide whether to respond or continue observing. Recently, Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO) has been applied to this setting, with existing approaches emphasizing reward design while retaining a single trajectory-level advantage across turns. Such shared credit can reinforce redundant replies after earlier successes and discourage useful follow-ups after earlier failures. We propose TurnWise, a turn-level GRPO framework that aligns learning with individual streaming decisions. TurnWise addresses the mismatch between trajectory-level feedback and turn-level interaction, promoting informative responses and reducing redundancy. Specifically, turn-level credit assignment uses silence-completed prefix scoring to construct group-relative advantages from each decision's remaining return, separating its learning signal from earlier rewards. Turn-mean policy optimization averages token losses within each turn and then across turns, giving response and waiting decisions equal weight regardless of output length. These complementary designs improve streaming policy learning without a learned critic or additional rollouts. Extensive experiments show our method outperforms prior RL approaches for streaming video understanding.

</details>

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-language-action models are typically built on a simple premise: perception is first compressed by visual encoders, and action reasoning begins only afterwards. ## 7. RACE: Calibrated Cumulative Scoring for Rollout-Free Robot Policy Checkpoint Selection
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.05838v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.05838v1
- Authors: Changyeon Kim, Yekyung Nah, Kimin Lee
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: robot policy

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Assessing the deployment performance of learned robot policies during training is challenging, as directly measuring task success requires costly real-world trials for each candidate checkpoint. Averaged validation loss offers a rollout-free alternative, but its checkpoint rankings can be inconsistent with deployment performance. We analyze two limitations of this criterion: it penalizes action deviations without considering whether the policy can recover from them, and gives later reference predictions equal weight even when earlier deviations may lead execution away from the reference. We propose RACE, a novel rollout-free metric for ranking checkpoints using a fixed set of held-out successful trajectories. RACE calibrates action deviations against state-conditional thresholds derived from action differences at similar states in these trajectories. We aggregate these scores through a running product, so that a substantial deviation reduces the contribution of subsequent reference predictions. Averaging the resulting cumulative scores over time and across trajectories yields the checkpoint score. In simulation, RACE outperforms baselines across validation sources, while several baselines degrade sharply when the validation set changes. On real robots, its rankings align with deployment success across the evaluated policy backbones and tasks of varying difficulty. Perturbation analyses further show that RACE distinguishes deviations with markedly different effects on task success, even when baselines respond similarly.

</details>

## 11. Compounding Physical RSI: Turning Execution Failure into Inherited Capability
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06654v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06654v1
- Authors: Yixuan Yang, Qiang Chen, Run Shao, Shuwei Dong, Wenhao Li, Yunpeng Wang, Wenhan Fang, Haomiao Wu...
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Generalist robot policies are trained on static offline datasets, yet physical manipulation unfolds sequentially across stages. Because a weakness in a downstream stage remains unobservable until the policy passes earlier ones, each resolved bottleneck exposes the next hidden failure mode. This staged structure calls for Recursive Self-Improvement (RSI), in which a policy iteratively executes, diagnoses failures, and updates itself. Moreover, because task completion behaves like a product of stage-level pass rates, recursive gains can multiply across generations. In physical environments, three bottlenecks obstruct compounding: terminal execution feedback rarely localizes the stage that caused a failure, failed trajectories do not supply the corrective action, and updating shared parameters can degrade previously mastered skills. First, automated capability-gap assessment traces failed rollouts to the task stage where progression breaks down and formulates a structured improvement objective. Second, conditioned on this objective, our remedy stage curates validated, stage-relevant experience and refines the policy via behavior-regularized offline reinforcement learning, without requiring costly online rollouts at each optimization step. Third, to safeguard existing skills, our retention stage constructs weight-space candidates between the inherited reference policy and the refined policy, evaluates them across the task suite, and promotes the best candidate as the reference state for the next generation. Evaluated across all 42 tasks of the RoboDojo-Sim benchmark, CSU-AI-07 ranks first on the leaderboard with a three-seed mean Overall Score of 26.15 and Success Rate of 20.70%, outperforming the next-best entry by 5.92 points and 5.82 percentage points.

## 12. Using Experience Twice: Dynamic Process Rubrics for Privileged Token-Level Credit Assignment
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2611.06543v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2611.06543v1
- Authors: Shumao Wu, Kongcheng Zhang, Jianqiang Huang, Baisheng Lai
- Keyword score: 1 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Agent trajectories often contain both successful decisions and failed attempts, yet optimization with sparse trajectory-level rewards struggles to identify which critical decisions should be reinforced or suppressed. We observe that meaningful distinctions among on-policy trajectories can be distilled into natural-language process rubrics and used as process supervision signals. Based on this insight, we propose Online Rubric Self-Distillation (ORSD), which leverages self-derived process rubrics for dense token-level supervision, thereby augmenting outcome-centric reinforcement learning. Specifically, the current policy independently generates multiple rubric bundles at each step according to the same group of trajectories, extracting candidate process rubrics from cross-trajectory behavioral differences. A frozen verifier then evaluates all rubric items against each trajectory in the group, and selects one candidate bundle based on bundle-level alignment between rubric satisfaction and task success. ORSD treats the selected rubric bundle as privileged information during training, then compares the log-likelihoods of the original tokens with and without the rubric. The resulting likelihood gap provides token-specific process credit, which is added to the original trajectory-level GRPO advantage through additive advantage shaping. ORSD thus bridges trajectory-level outcome supervision and fine-grained process supervision, enabling token-level credit assignment using process signals. Extensive experiments on ALFWorld and WebShop show that ORSD accelerates policy improvement and boosts final task performance for both Qwen3-1.7B and Qwen3-4B under the same environment-interaction budget. Our code is available at https://github.com/AnnaXChun/ORSD.
