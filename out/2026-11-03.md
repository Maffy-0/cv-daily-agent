## 1. DUGO: Dual-Scale Gaussian Memory for Online Temporal Action Localization in Egocentric Procedural Videos
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41402v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41402v1
- Authors: Mohammed Aman Bhuiyan, Md. Sazzad Hossain Adib, Samiul Basir Bhuiyan, Mir Faiyaz Hossain, Syed Mohammed Shamsul Islam, Md Zakir Hossain, Shafin Rahman
- Keyword score: 5 / hits: temporal action, streaming, real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Online Temporal Action Localization (OnTAL) aims to detect and localize action instances from untrimmed streaming videos in real time. Unlike offline approaches, OnTAL models have no access to future frames and cannot go back and refine earlier predictions, which makes the task considerably harder given the strict real-time constraints and narrow temporal window available. Most existing OnTAL methods lean on either brief anchor-based context or a single-scale memory of past frames, which limits how well they can capture actions unfolding across multiple time scales or requiring long-range reasoning in Procedural Videos. To address these gaps, we present DUGO (Dual-Scale Encoding and Gaussian Memory Online), a new OnTAL framework that jointly models local motion dynamics and long-range temporal dependencies. At its core, DUGO uses a Dual-Scale Temporal Encoder, pairing depth-wise local representations with global self-attention so it can pick up both fine-grained temporal patterns and broader contextual cues. We also introduce a Gaussian Memory Bank, which represents action memory as stochastic, input-conditioned Gaussian prototypes, allowing for richer temporal memory through gated cross-attention applied at both the encoder and anchor-query stages. Extensive experiments on procedural egocentric datasets (EGTEA, EPIC-Kitchens-100) demonstrate that DUGO achieves state-of-the-art performance while also providing substantial improvements on non-egocentric benchmarks such as Muses. The project page is available at: https://aman554-eq.github.io/DUGO

## 3. ART of PIV: Agentic Real-Time Particle Image Velocimetry for Active Flow Control
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41304v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41304v1
- Authors: Antonio Terpin, Francesco Banelli, Alan Bonomi, Raffaello D'Andrea
- Keyword score: 4 / hits: real-time, reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Particle image velocimetry (PIV) measures fluid motion from images of tracer particles, but its accuracy depends on choices throughout the estimation pipeline, including image preprocessing, flow estimation, and postprocessing. These choices are typically left to expert tuning, and a fixed configuration can become less accurate as conditions change. This matters especially when PIV supplies feedback for real-time control, where measurements must be both accurate and fast, and flow conditions vary. We introduce ART of PIV -- short for Agentic Real-Time and also a nod to the art of tuning a PIV pipeline -- which automates pipeline choice for each image pair. A learned agent selects a configuration according to a chosen measurement objective from a library assembled for a specified speed budget. Across cross-correlation, classical optical-flow, and learning-based estimators, ART of PIV reduces average end-point error (AEPE) by 12.2-22.4 % relative to the best single estimator at matched speed, with gains extending to 1000-4000 image pair/s. In a simulated local velocity regulation task, it reduces mean normalized closed-loop cost by 13.24-13.59 percentage points relative to a tuned fixed estimator, with the same classical controller in both cases. In a tabletop water channel, we illustrate two ways to tune ART of PIV for a fluidic pinball of three rotating cylinders: selecting between complementary optical-flow tunings for accuracy, and selecting regions of interest with coherent rotation or shear using a common flow estimator. For the first variant, we train a DreamerV3 reinforcement learning controller directly in the physical world and demonstrate tracking of a prescribed flow field in the wake of a fluidic pinball.

## 4. REA: A Routed Embodied Agent Runtime for Asynchronous Brain-Cerebellum Control
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.42053v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.42053v1
- Authors: Pengfei Zhou, Yixing Ma, Zhengfeiyang Zhang, Haoyuan Chen, Zhenglin Wan, Hexin Wang, Wangbo Zhao, Yang You
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: embodied agent

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Foundation models are becoming remarkably reliable at high-level reasoning, yet they remain too slow and costly to serve as fine-grained controllers. Conversely, fast policies like VLAs and whole-body controllers offer more efficient reflexes, but struggle to generalize. While pairing a deliberative brain (foundation models) with a reactive cerebellum (fast policies) seems natural for embodied systems, two obstacles remain: methods are built and scored on incompatible stacks, and their asynchronous coordination is difficult. For example, an agent that calls the VLA in the agentic loop can stall the fast execution, while a VLA that calls the agent on errors tends to re-trigger fallbacks repeatedly or completely miss silent failures. Evaluated on 3,000 test episodes across 60 LIBERO-Pro tasks, REA reaches 95.9% success and beats the best single-agent baseline in its own policy pool (94.7%) while reducing control steps by 25% and control time by 17%. In a separate 100-episode RoboTwin 2.0 study, REA achieves 84% success with VLA policies and 86% with VLA+code, compared with 80% for the strongest single VLA baseline MEDex. These results demonstrate learned and programmatic policies operating within the same runtime. REA also transfers to BEHAVIOR-1K and a real Unitree G1 humanoid, achieving the highest observed success among the compared methods.

## 5. Optimizing Stochastic Policy Geometry for Few-Step Flow Reinforcement Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41949v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41949v1
- Authors: Zhuguanyu Wu, Congjia Chen, Ruihao Gong, Yang Yong, Shiqiao Gu, Yi Xu, Xianglong Liu
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Few-step flow models generate samples through a handful of large denoising transitions. Applying GRPO to such models requires introducing stochasticity into the denoising transitions, and when a single transition spans a wide range of noise levels, the choice of the resulting stochastic policy becomes a major factor in optimization. Recent constructions constrain the policy to preserve the target flow coefficients, but this constraint admits a family of policies and offers no criterion for choosing among them. We introduce Geo-FSRL (Geometry-Optimized Few-Step Reinforcement Learning), built on a selection principle: at a fixed conditioning state and timestep pair, choose the admissible policy that minimizes the conditional KL divergence induced by any given nonzero velocity-output update. Within a coefficient-preserving family of isotropic Gaussian transitions, this criterion reduces to minimizing a single scalar geometry factor that determines the conditional KL for every velocity-output update. The same factor governs velocity-space Fisher information and conditional transition log-likelihood-ratio variance. We derive the unique closed-form optimizer within this family, shared across all nonzero update magnitudes and directions, with transition coefficients determined by both the source and target noise levels. Because the minimized geometry factor still varies across timesteps, we further adopt gradient-scale matching from prior work and derive closed-form policy-gradient and KL weights that normalize explicit score scales and reference-KL curvature. Geo-FSRL trains on the original few-step schedule and retains the deterministic solver at inference. Our method achieves higher final reward scores than Flow-GRPO and Flow-CPS on both image generation and video generation models under the 4-step setting. Code is available at https://github.com/ModelTC/LightX2V/tree/main/lightx2v_rl

</details>

- What: 数ステップのフロー強化学習のための、確率的ポリシーの幾何学を最適化するGeo-FSRLを提案。

## 6. Chance-Constrained Inspection Viewpoint Sequencing for On-Orbit Servicing via Safe Reinforcement Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.41639v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.41639v1
- Authors: Chloé Carer, Adam Abdin
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Autonomous inspection of a target spacecraft is typically the initial step in on-orbit servicing. Planning close-proximity observation viewpoints involves a complex combinatorial problem constrained by coverage, fuel, and safety considerations. This paper models the viewpoint selection as a Constrained Markov Decision Process (CMDP) and applies Proximal Policy Optimization with Lagrangian relaxation to solve it. The system combines a high-level planner, which sequences camera viewpoints on an observation sphere around the target, with a low-level model that assesses each transfer using the Clohessy--Wiltshire (CW) equations for relative motion. The deterministic constraints on single-impulse reachability and $Δv$ cap are enforced directly on the action set. Safety margins are affected by navigation and manoeuvre errors, making the actual clearance stochastic. To address this, a chance constraint on the realised trajectory is implemented, with a Lagrangian multiplier governed by PID control to adapt the penalty during training. The approach is tested on a Galileo-class navigation satellite in medium Earth orbit, modelled as a point cloud where visibility from each viewpoint considers field-of-view and line-of-sight occlusion. Across various risk budgets, the trained policy maintains the keep-out violation rate within the specified limit, achieving coverage close to its maximum and staying within the mission $Δv$ budget. Compared to the usual practice of applying a fixed margin to the keep-out radius and planning deterministically, the chance-constrained policy attains less than half the violation rate at comparable coverage. This makes the collision risk an explicit design parameter, eliminating the need for re-tuning per case. The learned policy selects a viewpoint without the repeated Monte Carlo risk estimates required by a greedy, chance-constrained planner at every decision point.

</details>

## 7. SACQ: Structure-Aware Codebook Quantization for Ultra-Low-Bit Vision-Language-Action Models

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models enable capable robot behavior, but their large memory footprint limits deployment on resource-constrained robotic platforms. Weight quantization is a natural remedy, yet post-training quantization (PTQ) recipes inherited from large language models degrade when applied to VLAs at ultra-low precision. <details><summary>Abstract</summary>
