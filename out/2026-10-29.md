## 1. VLA-Stem: A Lightweight Safety Mechanism for Vision-Language-Action Models via Reinforcement Learning
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37378v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37378v1
- Authors: Bojun Zou, Wenhao Yu, Jiaohao Li, Jiaxuan Lin, Guanting Ye, Haohan Geng, Weihao Yu, Yanyong Zhang...
- Keyword score: 6 / hits: vision-language-action, reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Vision-Language-Action (VLA) models integrate high-level reasoning with low-level action execution for end-to-end robotic control. However, most VLA models target static manipulation in unconstrained environments, overlooking spatial constraints and dynamic obstacles. Fine-tuning VLAs on obstacle-avoidance data is a straightforward solution, but collecting such data is costly, especially for dynamic scenarios, and may degrade their original task capabilities. To address these limitations, we propose VLA-Stem, a lightweight, reinforcement-learning-based safety mechanism for VLA systems. Without fine-tuning the underlying VLA policy, VLA-Stem converts its action chunks into motion references and uses trajectory-level guidance to preserve their spatial intent while allowing flexible motion and timing adjustments. Conditioned on these references, robot proprioception, and local obstacle information, the Stem policy adaptively modifies execution at inference time, preserving the original task intent while enabling responsive adaptation to spatial constraints and dynamic obstacles. For robust and sample-efficient training, we combine teacher-student distillation with curriculum learning. We evaluate VLA-Stem on reference following, static spatial constraints, and dynamic obstacle avoidance. Across six constrained simulation settings, VLA-Stem achieves 52.8% average collision-free task success versus 23.7% for the strongest safety baseline, while preserving base-policy performance in obstacle-free settings; real-world experiments further show consistent collision reductions under static and dynamic obstacles. These results demonstrate that VLA-Stem couples high-level VLA task intent with rapid, reflex-like responses to immediate physical risks, providing a brainstem-inspired mechanism for robust robot execution.

## 2. Gym-Anything-RT: Scaling Real-Time Environments for Vision-Language Agents
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37567v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37567v1
- Authors: Pranjal Aggarwal, Sean Welleck
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: real-time

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

AI agents have made substantial progress in domains where they have a non-trivial amount of time to deliberate between actions, such as mathematics, software engineering, and performing tasks on desktop applications. It is less clear how such models fare in settings that demand real-time interaction, such as playing a video game or operating a physical device. In these settings, the environment state may evolve as the agent is reasoning, or even as the underlying model is performing a forward pass. To gain insight into the real-time interaction capabilities of vision-language agents, we introduce Gym-Anything-RT. Our key idea is to construct visual minigames that demand a portfolio of capabilities required for interactive real-time settings. Gym-Anything-RT uses an agentic pipeline to automatically generate such environments based on a taxonomy of six capabilities required for real-time interaction, including the ability to perceive the environment and act under an evolving environment state. A key feature of Gym-Anything-RT is that it allows for controlled comparison of real-time interaction capabilities: we can run the same minigame in real time, or pause or slow down the environment to relax the requirement for real-time actions. We use Gym-Anything-RT to construct 100 real-time minigames and evaluate AI agents on them. We find large capability gaps in real-time interaction. For example, GPT-6 Astra (xHigh) achieves a pass rate of only 9.0% on the real-time minigames, which rises to 75.0% when the environment is paused. We also identify intriguing behaviors by the most powerful frontier agents: for instance, when given the ability to write code, these agents write code that bypasses the need to perceive and act in real time. We publicly release Gym-Anything-RT and the generated minigames to spur further research on AI agents in real-time interactive settings.

## 3. ReferStream: Interactive Subject Injection for Streaming Video Generation
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37566v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37566v1
- Authors: Shuolin Xu, Guanyuan Li, Boyu Li, Kelu Yao, Xiaogang Xu, Hongchuan Yu, Shuicheng Yan, Xiaobin Hu
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: streaming

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Injecting new subjects naturally during streaming video generation requires integrating newly supplied visual references into the existing video while preserving established subjects and the scene. Encoding references independently of the current scene provides little guidance for this integration, while keeping their features in persistent memory can interfere with established context. We propose ReferStream, a framework that requires no additional training and combines reference construction using the current scene with transient cache injection. Joint Latent places reference latents in target regions of the latest scene latent, allowing reference features to be extracted in context. Cache Restoration uses these features to condition the injection chunk, then restores the overwritten global cache entries while retaining the newly generated history. This procedure incorporates new visual conditions without retraining the generator or revising emitted frames. We further introduce ReferStream Bench, a new benchmark with scheduled reference arrivals, evolving instructions, and explicit annotations of subject presence for InShot and MultiShot generation. Evaluation follows the event schedule and measures both the fidelity of new subjects and the preservation of established subjects, alongside video quality, text adherence, and count accuracy. Experiments on ReferStream Bench show higher scores for text adherence, subject similarity, and count accuracy than the compared baselines, including offline methods given all references in advance. Project page: https://xushuolin.github.io/ReferStream/.

## 5. Vision-Language-Action or World-Action? Just Action Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37219v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37219v1
- Authors: Yonghao Yu, Lang Huang, Runyi Li, Toshihiko Yamasaki
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: vision-language-action

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

The vision-language-action (VLA) and world-action model (WAM) communities debate whether control policies should inherit pretrained knowledge from vision-language models or from visual generators. The debate, however, bundles three separable choices: backbone initialization, the objective applied to future observations, and whether the machinery for that future loss is kept at inference. A gap between a VLA and a WAM therefore cannot simply be attributed to any one of them. We show that decoupling these choices yields a common framework for both families, which we term Just Action Model (JAM): a pretrained visual backbone with an action expert, trained with an action loss and a foresight loss. The foresight loss trains current-observation features to predict frozen encodings of the recorded future through a prediction head discarded after training, so no visual generation is involved in either training or inference, and the backbone can be chosen freely. We first compare objectives within fixed policy architectures, then evaluate the foresight recipe across backbone families. The resulting JAM policies outperform their matched FastWAM and ImageWAM counterparts on LIBERO and LIBERO-Plus, and achieve similar or slightly higher success rates on RoboTwin. Compared with FastWAM, JAM improves the success rates from 97.0%/55.3%/91.2% to 98.2%/64.5%/91.4%, respectively. With cached foresight targets, JAM reduces estimated online training FLOPs per sample per update by 73% relative to FastWAM. Our results show that future supervision improves control without visual generation or additional inference machinery, and strong foresight performance is attainable with either generator-initialized or vision-language backbones. ## 6. Live-OPSD: On-Policy Self-Distillation for Proactive Streaming Large Video Models
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.36959v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36959v1
- Authors: Kaixuan Du, Xin Wan, Dai Guan, Ming Chen, Xiaoxi Jiang, Guanjun Jiang
- Keyword score: 3 / hits: streaming

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Streaming multimodal assistants must make response decisions while events are still unfolding. They need to recognize when the available evidence warrants a response, produce an appropriate continuation, and remain silent otherwise. Supervised fine-tuning learns from recorded assistant turns, but does not directly train on the response prefixes produced by the evolving policy itself. We introduce Live-OPSD, an on-policy self-distillation method that uses privileged hints available only during training to supervise the policy on its own outputs. The policy first generates a continuation from the streaming context alone. The same model then re-evaluates that continuation with a hint describing the relevant evidence, and the resulting distributions guide the unhinted policy. On Qwen3-Omni-30B-A3B, Live-OPSD attains an average score of 59.64 on LiveProBench, 5.26 points above the strongest continued-SFT baseline, and stays competitive on OVO-Bench (65.84). These results indicate that guiding the policy on its own outputs with privileged hints improves both when and how a streaming assistant responds.

## 7. Hyperparameter and Neural Architecture Search via Evolutionary Optimization for Reinforcement Learning: HPNAS-RL

- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.36882v1

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

In this study, we propose a dual-branch evolutionary framework that simultaneously performs Neural Architecture Search (NAS) and Hyperparameter Optimization (HPO) for Reinforcement Learning (RL) in Autonomous Driving (AD) tasks. While NAS has shown strong potential in automating architecture design for Convolutional Neural Networks (CNNs), its application to RL remains limited, especially in scenarios that require joint optimization of architectural and training dynamics. Building on our prior work on Evolutionary Multi-Objective NAS (EMNAS), the proposed framework evolves discrete network architectures and continuous hyperparameters in parallel under a unified multi-objective search strategy. In this setup, NAS targets the convolutional layers of the RL policy network - responsible for visual feature extraction, while HPO focuses on optimizing critical RL hyperparameters. To accelerate convergence and improve generalization, we incorporate parallel evaluation and a teacher-student policy transfer mechanism. Experiments in a high-fidelity driving simulator show that the joint NAS-HPO framework consistently outperforms standalone NAS, yielding policies with higher rewards, fewer parameters, and improved stability. These results highlight the benefits of jointly optimizing model structure and training dynamics for scalable and effective RL agent design.

## 10. VideoLVR: Latent Visual Reasoning over Grounded Video Evidence
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37613v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37613v1
- Authors: Shilong Li, Xingyuan Bu, Hao Shi, Yun Xing, Weixin Feng, Qiangpeng Yang, Shilei Wen
- Keyword score: 2 / hits: video understanding, reinforcement learning

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Chain-of-thought reasoning has substantially advanced video understanding in multimodal large language models (MLLMs). However, language-based reasoning traces do not provide a reliable mechanism for models to revisit relevant visual evidence, leading to visual forgetting and impairing their ability to reason over video content. Existing approaches maintain visual grounding during reasoning in two main ways: they either refer to evidence using textual coordinates, which specify symbolic locations rather than the visual content itself, or reinsert cropped regions or frames into the multimodal context, preserving visual details at the cost of substantial context expansion. To address these limitations, we introduce VideoLVR, which constructs latent visual evidence from local regions at relevant video moments, thereby providing visual support for intermediate reasoning steps. To supervise latent visual evidence generation, we introduce a visual token prediction objective that formulates visual token reconstruction as prediction over a vocabulary derived from the input video, thereby unifying visual evidence reconstruction and text generation within the same token prediction framework. This shared prediction framework enables MLLMs to reason over interleaved text tokens and latent visual states while integrating evidence from different moments. We further extend VideoLVR to reinforcement learning by treating sampled visual tokens as actions alongside text tokens and jointly optimizing the policy using final-answer rewards. By efficiently revisiting task-relevant visual information during reasoning, VideoLVR improves video understanding without substantially expanding the multimodal context. Experiments on multiple video benchmarks demonstrate its effectiveness, especially on tasks requiring fine-grained visual evidence.

## 13. PerceptRL: Making Visual Perception Rewardable in MLLMs from Answers to Interactions
- arXiv: http://arxiv.org/abs/2610.37471v1
- PDF: https://arxiv.org/pdf/2610.37471v1

<details><summary>Abstract</summary>

Fine-grained visual perception remains a bottleneck during post-training of multimodal large language models (MLLMs), as perceptual errors can constrain gains from reasoning-focused optimization. Although image-caption pre-training improves perception, its influence can weaken as subsequent stages change data mixtures and objectives. Continuing caption supervision during post-training is challenging because subjective, information-dense descriptions complicate verifiable reward design. We present PerceptRL, which strengthens perception at two complementary levels. Perception RL uses a multi-agent pipeline to extract verified errors from dense image captions and convert the corresponding visual facts into QA pairs with fixed answer labels and rule-based rewards. Chain QA links correlated errors, while multiple-choice QA combines unrelated errors. A specialized route targets high-error domains with tailored verification, and a domain-agnostic general route covers errors beyond these domains. Perception Agentic RL addresses fine-grained and dense cases unresolved by single-turn Perception RL through multi-turn inspection with visual tools. We observe coordinate drift even in models trained to use tools, where incorrect crops or annotations distort evidence and propagate errors.
